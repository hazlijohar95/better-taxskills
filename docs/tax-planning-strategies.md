# Malaysian Corporate Tax Planning Strategies — Practical Guide

**Legislative basis:** Income Tax Act 1967 (ITA); Promotion of Investments Act 1986 (PIA); Labuan Business Activity Tax Act 1990 (LBATA)
**Key LHDN Public Rulings cited:** PR 01/2022, PR 5/2022, PR 6/2016, PR 04/2019, PR 10/2014, PR 03/2021, PR 4/2013, PR 10/2020, PR 10/2022, PR 13/2022
**Effective:** YA 2025/2026 (includes Budget 2026 measures tabled 10 October 2025)

---

## STAGE 1: LOSS-MAKING COMPANIES

### 1.1 Maximising Value of Tax Losses (10-Year Carry Forward)

**Legal basis:** Subsection 44(5F), ITA — effective YA 2019, unabsorbed adjusted business losses can be carried forward for a maximum of 10 consecutive YAs.

**Key rules:**
- Losses arising in YA 2019: must be fully absorbed by YA 2029, or disregarded from YA 2030
- Pre-2019 accumulated losses: carry forward until YA 2028 (Section 11, Finance Act 2018 [Act 812])
- The clock starts from the YA the loss ARISES, not from when you start carrying it forward

**Practical strategies:**

| Strategy | How | ITA Reference |
|----------|-----|---------------|
| Accelerate income into loss years | Invoice earlier, recognise progress billing, trigger revenue recognition events | Section 24 (basis of assessment) |
| Defer discretionary capital allowances | Preserve losses by NOT claiming CA in loss years — CA has no time limit | Section 42, Schedule 3 |
| Use current-year loss against ALL statutory income first | Current year loss offsets all sources (rental, interest, etc.) under s.44(2) BEFORE carry forward | Subsection 44(2) |
| Plan FYE to create a short basis period in transition year | Shorter loss year = less clock time consumed per loss quantum | Section 21A |
| Convert capital items to revenue where legitimately possible | Revenue losses have more immediate offset power than CA | General principles |

**Critical warning:** Once the 10-year clock expires, losses are permanently DISREGARDED — they cannot be revived, even if the company becomes profitable in year 11.

### 1.2 Group Relief (Section 44A)

**What it is:** A surrendering company can transfer up to 70% of its adjusted loss to a related claimant company within the same group.

**The severe limitations (PR 6/2016):**

| Condition | Implication |
|-----------|-------------|
| Surrendering company must be in first 3 YAs of business | Only useful for new subsidiaries |
| 70% ordinary share ownership between companies | Through Malaysian-incorporated intermediaries only |
| Both must have paid-up capital > RM2.5 million | Excludes most SMEs |
| Same accounting period | Exact same FYE required |
| Neither company enjoying any incentive | No PS, ITA, RA, or any other incentive |
| Maximum 70% of adjusted loss surrenderable | 30% permanently trapped in surrendering company |
| Only ADJUSTED LOSS (not unabsorbed CA) | CA cannot be transferred between companies |

**Practical planning for group relief:**
1. When establishing a new subsidiary expected to make losses in early years, DELIBERATELY structure it to meet all s.44A conditions from day 1
2. Set the new subsidiary's FYE to match the profitable parent/sister company's FYE
3. Capitalise the new subsidiary at > RM2.5 million paid-up capital (even if cash is returned as shareholder loan)
4. Ensure 70% ownership is maintained throughout
5. Do NOT apply for any tax incentive for either company during the group relief period

**Real-world use case:** Large manufacturing group establishes new subsidiary for R&D activities. Expected 3-year loss period. Group relief transfers 70% of losses to profitable manufacturing parent. Remaining 30% stays in subsidiary for future use.

### 1.3 Entity Conversion Assessment

**Sole proprietor to Sdn Bhd (when in losses):**
- Generally NOT advisable during loss period because:
  - Sole prop losses can offset the individual's OTHER income (employment, rental, etc.) under s.44(2)
  - Sdn Bhd losses are trapped within the company
  - Conversion crystallises balancing charges on assets transferred (unless continued by sole prop turned company under s.37)
- Exception: Convert IF the business will be profitable within 2-3 years AND the long-term corporate structure benefits (limited liability, SME rates) outweigh the loss of immediate offset

**Sdn Bhd to sole prop (when in losses):**
- Almost never advisable because:
  - Striking off the company triggers final tax computation, losses lost permanently
  - No mechanism to transfer corporate losses to individual
  - May trigger balancing charges on assets

### 1.4 Preserving Losses Through Ownership Changes

**The shareholder continuity rule (Subsection 44(5A)-(5D), ITA):**

> Unabsorbed adjusted losses carried forward shall be DISREGARDED unless the DG is satisfied that the shareholders on the last day of the basis period in which the loss would be deducted are **substantially the same** as the shareholders on the first day of that basis period.

**"Substantially the same"** is NOT defined precisely in the ITA. LHDN interprets this as MORE THAN 50% of the shareholders (by value of shareholding) remaining the same.

**Practical implications:**
- If you sell/transfer > 50% of shares in a loss-making company, all carried-forward losses are FORFEITED
- This applies to EVERY year you want to use the losses — the test is applied annually
- Corporate shareholders: the look-through rule (s.44(5C)) deems shares held by a company to be held by ITS shareholders — prevents circumvention via interposing companies
- The Minister has power to exempt under "special circumstances" (s.44(5D)) — rarely granted in practice

**Strategies to preserve losses:**
1. Stage share transfers over multiple years, keeping > 50% continuity at each year-end
2. If restructuring is necessary, complete it AFTER all losses are fully utilised
3. Use share subscription (new shares) rather than transfer of existing shares where possible — new shares dilute but don't necessarily trigger the continuity test if original shareholders retain majority
4. Document commercial rationale for any shareholding change (strengthens case if LHDN challenges)

**Para 75A-75C (Schedule 3) — CA continuity:**
- Same shareholder continuity test applies to unabsorbed capital allowances
- The Minister may exempt under Para 75C in "special circumstances"

### 1.5 Capital Allowance Strategies in Loss Position

**Key principle:** Unabsorbed CA has NO TIME LIMIT for carry forward (unlike losses which have 10 years). This creates a critical strategic decision.

| Situation | Recommended Strategy | Rationale |
|-----------|---------------------|-----------|
| Company has both losses and unabsorbed CA | Utilise losses FIRST (claim minimum CA) | Losses expire in 10 years; CA never expires |
| Company approaching year 10 of loss carry forward | Maximise CA claims to preserve the loss quantum | Use CA to absorb income; losses then carried forward |
| New capital expenditure in loss years | Claim ONLY initial allowance (mandatory) | Annual allowance can be deferred by election |

**Small value assets (Para 19A, Schedule 3):**
- In a loss position: Do NOT claim small value asset write-off
- Why: SVA gives 100% immediate write-off — wastes the deduction in a year with no income
- Instead: Claim normal IA (20%) + AA (14%) over time, matching CA to future profitable years
- Exception: If the loss will definitely be utilised within 10 years anyway, SVA timing is irrelevant

**However:** The election for capital allowance is per asset. Once you claim it one way, you cannot change. Consider the 10-year loss clock carefully.

### 1.6 Timing Strategies

**Deferring income (legal methods):**
- Delay invoicing where contractually permissible (do NOT fabricate — must have commercial reason)
- For service businesses: structure contracts with milestone billing, defer milestone completion
- For property developers: defer completion of units to shift income to later periods
- For manufacturers: manage production scheduling to control inventory vs sales timing

**Accelerating expenses:**
- Bring forward repairs and maintenance to current basis period
- Pay annual subscriptions/insurance in advance (deductible in period PAID for revenue items)
- Settle outstanding staff bonuses before FYE (deduction in period bonus becomes payable)
- Prepay professional fees for current-period services

**Warning:** Artificial arrangements solely to shift timing are vulnerable to Section 140 (general anti-avoidance). Ensure every timing decision has commercial substance.

### 1.7 The Dormancy Trap

**What happens:**
- A dormant company has no business activity, no gross income
- If dormant for extended period, LHDN may deem the business to have ceased
- Upon cessation: all unabsorbed losses and CA are crystallised and potentially lost
- Per PR 03/2021: a dormant company with no gross income from business sources cannot claim SVA
- Per LHDN practice: if a company makes loans to directors while dormant, it is deemed to have commenced operations under s.21A(8)

**How to avoid the trap:**
1. Maintain MINIMAL business activity (even nominal — e.g., management services to group, small consulting income)
2. Continue filing Form C annually — even if nil return
3. Keep the company "resident" — ensure at least one board meeting in Malaysia per year
4. Do NOT let the company's SSM registration lapse
5. If the company has valuable losses, consider whether minimal inter-company transactions (at arm's length) can generate just enough activity to prevent cessation

## STAGE 2: BREAKEVEN COMPANIES

### 2.1 Capital Expenditure Timing — Budget 2026 ACA Window

**The opportunity:** QCE incurred between 11 October 2025 and 31 December 2026 qualifies for accelerated capital allowance (full write-off within ~2 years: 20% IA + 40% AA).

**What qualifies:**
- Heavy machinery, plant, general machinery from LOCAL manufacturers
- ICT equipment and computer software
- Software development/customisation costs

**Strategy for breakeven companies:**
- If you were planning capex for early 2027, PULL IT FORWARD to December 2026
- The difference: 2-year write-off vs potentially 7-14 years under normal rates
- Even if you cannot fully absorb the CA immediately, unabsorbed CA carries forward indefinitely
- Consider whether to FINANCE the purchase rather than delay — the interest cost may be less than the NPV of the accelerated deduction

### 2.2 CA Claim vs Preservation Decision

**The asymmetry that matters:**
| Item | Time Limit |
|------|-----------|
| Unabsorbed adjusted losses | 10 years from YA of loss |
| Unabsorbed capital allowances | INDEFINITE (no time limit) |

**Decision framework for breakeven companies:**

```
IF company has both losses approaching expiry AND current-year income:
  → CLAIM MAXIMUM CA this year
  → This creates/increases unabsorbed CA (infinite shelf life)
  → Simultaneously, use current income to absorb the expiring losses

IF company has no expiring losses but uncertain future profitability:
  → DEFER CA claims (claim minimum — IA only is mandatory if asset used)
  → Preserve CA for years when income materialises
  → CA deferred = effectively an indefinite "tax credit" waiting to be used

IF company expects profits from next year onwards:
  → CLAIM CA NOW to create tax losses
  → Losses created from CA excess over income carry forward 10 years
  → This effectively converts infinite-life CA into 10-year-life losses
  → Only beneficial if you're confident of profits within 10 years
```

**Important:** Annual allowance under Schedule 3 is NOT automatic. A person may elect NOT to claim AA for a year of assessment (PR 12/2014). Initial allowance, however, is given in the year the asset is first used — it cannot be deferred.

### 2.3 R&D Double Deduction Timing

**Legal basis:** Section 34A — 200% deduction for approved R&D expenditure

**Timing considerations:**
- Double deduction DOUBLES the loss if in a loss year — but the resulting loss is still subject to the 10-year limit
- Better to time R&D expenditure recognition in a year with some income, so the double deduction creates a PLANNED loss with maximum utilisation time ahead
- If at breakeven: the 200% deduction on even modest R&D spend tips the company into a loss, which then has 10 years to be absorbed
- MOSTI approval is required for in-house R&D — plan applications 6+ months ahead

### 2.4 Renovation Allowance — Claim vs Defer

**General R&R Allowance:**
- Cap: RM300,000 per 3-year period
- Deducted from adjusted income (like CA)
- If not fully utilised: depends on specific gazette order terms (some allow carry forward, some do not)

**Strategy:** For a breakeven company, if renovation is planned:
- Check whether the 3-year window resets soon — if yes, DEFER renovation to the new window
- If renovation creates unabsorbed allowance that cannot carry forward, DEFER the renovation to a year with sufficient income
- Tourism operators: The Budget 2026 RM500,000 cap (11 Oct 2025 - 31 Dec 2027) is significantly higher — time expenditure within this window

### 2.5 Expense vs Capitalise Decision

| Factor | Expense (Immediate Deduction) | Capitalise (CA Over Time) |
|--------|-------------------------------|---------------------------|
| Timing of benefit | Immediate | Spread over 3-14 years |
| If in loss position | Increases loss (10-year limit applies) | Creates CA (indefinite carry forward) |
| At breakeven | Wipes out income immediately | Preserves some income for other reliefs |
| Cash flow | Tax saving NOW (if profitable) | Tax saving LATER |
| Documentary risk | May be challenged as capital | Generally accepted if genuinely capital |

**Practical examples:**
- Website development: Revenue or capital? If maintaining existing site = revenue. If building new platform with enduring benefit = capital. At breakeven, capitalise to spread deduction.
- Professional fees for restructuring: Revenue if relating to existing business operations. Capital if creating new structure/entity.
- Software: Licence fee (recurring) = revenue. Perpetual licence = capital (but Budget 2026 ACA applies)

### 2.6 Staff Bonus Timing

**The rule (Section 33(1) + case law):**
- Bonuses are deductible in the basis period in which the LIABILITY to pay arises (i.e., when "incurred")
- A bonus ACCRUED at year-end is deductible if there is a DEFINITE obligation to pay
- LHDN's position: the bonus must be paid within a reasonable time after the basis period ends — otherwise, it may be treated as a provision (not deductible)
- General practice: bonus accrued at FYE and paid within 2-3 months after FYE is accepted as "incurred"

**Strategies for breakeven companies:**
- If approaching FYE with unexpected profits: declare and accrue bonuses before year-end (creates immediate deduction)
- If in loss: DEFER bonus declaration to subsequent year when income arises
- Document board resolution for bonus (date of resolution = date liability crystallises)
- Ensure payment within reasonable time of accrual to avoid LHDN challenge

### 2.7 Bad Debt Crystallisation

**Legal basis:** Subsection 34(2), PR 04/2019

**Conditions for deduction:**
1. Debt must have been included in gross income of a prior year
2. Debt is reasonably estimated to be wholly/partly irrecoverable
3. All reasonable recovery steps have been taken
4. Formal write-off in accounting records

**Timing strategy:**
- If at breakeven with bad debts pending: CRYSTALLISE the write-off NOW (board resolution + accounting entry + evidence of recovery attempts)
- This creates or increases a loss with 10-year carry forward
- If expecting profits next year: consider whether to write off THIS year (loss carry forward) or NEXT year (direct offset against income)
- Remember: recovered bad debts previously written off are TAXABLE in year of recovery (s.30(1))

**Evidence required (PR 04/2019 para 5.3.2):**
- Debtor deceased with no estate
- Debtor bankrupt/in liquidation with no assets
- Debt is statute-barred
- Amount too small to justify legal action costs
- Debtor cannot be traced despite reasonable efforts
- Court judgment obtained but cannot be enforced

## STAGE 3: PROFITABLE COMPANIES

### 3.1 SME Rate Optimisation

**Current rates (YA 2025):**
| Chargeable Income | Rate |
|-------------------|------|
| First RM150,000 | 15% |
| RM150,001 - RM600,000 | 17% |
| Above RM600,000 | 24% |

**ALL 5 conditions must be met (simultaneously):**

1. Paid-up capital of ordinary shares: **≤ RM2.5 million**
2. Gross income from business: **≤ RM50 million**
3. Not more than 20% of paid-up capital owned (directly or indirectly) by foreign shareholders
4. Not controlled by a company with paid-up capital > RM2.5 million (>50% ownership)
5. Does not control a company with paid-up capital > RM2.5 million (>50% ownership)

**Structuring strategies:**

| Objective | Strategy | Risk |
|-----------|----------|------|
| Keep paid-up ≤ RM2.5m | Issue shares at premium (share premium doesn't count) | None — legitimate |
| Avoid foreign ownership > 20% | Nominee arrangements NOT acceptable; restructure actual ownership | Must be genuine |
| Avoid control by/of large companies | Ensure no single large company holds > 50% | If group structure requires > 50%, SME rate is lost |
| Keep gross income ≤ RM50m | Split activities into separate companies (each < RM50m) | Section 140 risk if artificial |
| Multiple companies to multiply the RM600k band | Establish separate companies for genuinely separate businesses | Must have commercial substance |

**The multiplication strategy (controversial but common):**
- Company A: Manufacturing (chargeable income RM500k — taxed at 15%/17% blend)
- Company B: Trading/distribution (chargeable income RM400k — taxed at 15%/17% blend)
- Instead of one company with RM900k chargeable income (part taxed at 24%)
- LHDN will challenge if the split has NO commercial purpose — must demonstrate genuine separate businesses, separate customers, separate risk profiles, separate employees

### 3.2 Maximum Deduction Strategies

**Double deductions available:**
| Item | Effective Deduction | Key Requirement |
|------|-------------------|-----------------|
| Export promotion (overseas) | 200% | Overseas advertising, trade fairs, overseas office |
| R&D (in-house, MOSTI approved) | 200% | Formal MOSTI approval required |
| Employee training (approved) | 200% | Approved institutions/programmes |
| Disabled employee remuneration | 200% | JKM-registered employees |
| Halal certification | 200% | JAKIM/local religious authority |
| Environmental protection equipment | 200% | Per gazette orders |
| Internship | 200% | Structured programme with HEIs |
| Scholarships | 200% | Extended to bachelor's + professional quals in ICT/engineering/accounting/finance (Budget 2026) |

**Stacking strategy:** A profitable company should MAXIMISE all double deductions before considering any other planning:
- Example: RM100,000 spent on approved training = RM200,000 deduction = RM48,000 tax saving at 24% rate
- The effective cost of approved training is RM100,000 - RM48,000 = RM52,000 (i.e., government subsidises 48%)

### 3.3 Capital Allowance Acceleration

**Automation CA (IR 4.0):**
- 200% ACA on QCE up to RM10 million
- Company must be operating ≥ 36 months in manufacturing/services
- Equipment must incorporate at least one IR 4.0 element (AI, robotics, IoT, cloud, big data, etc.)
- Effective: YA 2023 to YA 2027
- Strategy: LABEL equipment purchases as "automation" where genuinely incorporating IR 4.0 elements — ensures 200% write-off vs normal 14% AA

**Budget 2026 ACA (11 Oct 2025 - 31 Dec 2026):**
- Full write-off in ~2 years (20% IA + 40% AA)
- Must be from LOCAL manufacturers (for heavy machinery/plant)
- ICT equipment and software have no local-sourcing requirement
- Strategy: Front-load ALL planned capex into this window

**Small value assets (Para 19A, Schedule 3):**
- SME companies (paid-up ≤ RM2.5m): NO cap on total SVA claims per year (each asset ≤ RM2,000)
- Non-SME companies: capped at RM20,000 per YA total
- Strategy for SMEs: disaggregate large purchases into component parts under RM2,000 each where genuinely separable (e.g., individual monitors, keyboards, chairs rather than "workstation package")
- WARNING: HP assets do NOT qualify for SVA

### 3.4 Donation Strategy

**Legal basis:** Subsection 44(6), (11B), (11C), (11D) — cap is 10% of aggregate income (from YA 2020, previously 7%)

**Qualifying donations:**
- Cash to approved institutions/organisations (s.44(6))
- Approved sports activities (s.44(11B))
- Approved arts/cultural projects (s.44(11C))
- Community projects (s.44(11D))
- Artefacts, manuscripts, paintings to government (s.44(6A))

**Timing strategy:**
- Calculate 10% of expected aggregate income BEFORE year-end
- If current donations are below the cap, make additional donations before FYE
- Excess donations (above 10%) are NOT carried forward — they are permanently lost
- Therefore: NEVER over-donate in one year; spread across years
- If expecting a particularly profitable year, maximise donations that year (higher cap = higher absolute deduction)

### 3.5 Zakat Optimisation

**For companies:** Zakat (business zakat) paid by a company is deductible against tax, capped at **2.5% of aggregate income** (subsection 6A(3) for rebate; various gazette orders for deduction).

**Important distinction:**
- Zakat paid by the COMPANY (not by individual shareholders on their dividends) is a deduction FROM aggregate income
- Unlike donations which are capped at 10%, zakat is a SEPARATE deduction
- Strategy: Pay zakat at the COMPANY level (where it reduces taxable income) rather than at the individual shareholder level (where it only provides personal tax rebate)
- Ensure zakat is paid to a state religious authority or approved body — payment to non-approved bodies is not deductible

### 3.6 Transfer Pricing — Legitimate Structuring

**Section 140A and TP Rules 2012:** All controlled transactions must be at arm's length.

**Legitimate intra-group charges (WITH proper documentation):**

| Service | Basis for Pricing | Documentation Required |
|---------|-------------------|----------------------|
| Management fees | Cost-plus or comparable independent service fees | TP documentation, service agreement, time records |
| Technical/licensing fees | CUP or comparable royalty rates | Licence agreement, market benchmarking |
| Shared services | Cost allocation (direct + indirect) with markup | Allocation keys, activity analysis |
| Intercompany loans | Market interest rate (benchmark against bank rates) | Loan agreement, evidence of market rates |
| Centralised purchasing | Savings passed through with appropriate margin | Purchase records, independent pricing |

**Strategy for profitable companies with related loss-making companies:**
- Ensure LEGITIMATE services flow from loss-makers to profit-makers
- Charge at arm's length (not inflated — LHDN will adjust and penalise)
- Maintain contemporaneous TP documentation (mandatory for controlled transactions)
- The effective result: shifts some taxable income to the loss-making entity where it is sheltered by losses
- WARNING: Must have genuine economic substance. LHDN's TP audit team is sophisticated and will reject arrangements without real services.

### 3.7 Financial Year End Change Strategies

**Legal basis:** Section 21A — company chooses its accounting period.

**How FYE change affects tax:**
- When changing FYE from (say) 31 December to 30 June, a STRADDLE period occurs
- The overlap formula (s.42(2)) reduces adjusted income for the overlap period: A x B/C
- Where A = adjusted income for new basis period, B = overlap length, C = new basis period length
- This can create a temporary tax reduction in the transition year

**When to change FYE:**
- Highly profitable year expected: change FYE to create short basis period (less income taxed in one YA)
- Loss expected soon: extend the basis period to push more income into the pre-loss period
- Seasonal business: align FYE with natural low point (inventory low, debtors collected, expenses accrued)

**Warning:** Frequent FYE changes attract LHDN scrutiny. Only one change in a 3-year period is generally accepted without questions. Section 140 (anti-avoidance) can apply if the sole purpose is tax reduction.

### 3.8 Directors' Remuneration vs Dividends

**The trade-off:**

| Factor | Directors' Remuneration | Dividends |
|--------|------------------------|-----------|
| Company deduction | YES (s.33(1)) — reduces corporate tax | NO deduction (paid from after-tax profits) |
| Individual tax | Taxed as employment income (0-30% scale) | Exempt (single-tier system from YA 2008) |
| EPF | Mandatory employer contribution (12-13%) | Not applicable |
| SOCSO/EIS | Applicable | Not applicable |
| Overall effective rate | Company saves 24% (or 15-17% for SME); individual pays 0-30% | Company pays 24% (or 15-17%); individual pays 0% |

**Optimal strategy:**
1. Pay directors sufficient remuneration to bring their personal income to the HIGHEST efficient marginal rate
2. For director-shareholders in the 24-30% personal bracket: dividends may be more efficient (0% vs 24-30%)
3. For director-shareholders in 0-24% personal bracket: remuneration is MORE efficient (company gets 24% deduction, individual pays < 24%)
4. The CROSSOVER POINT for YA 2025: chargeable income of approximately RM250,000 (where marginal rate = 24%)

**Worked example:**
- Company at 24% rate, director-shareholder in 13% personal bracket
- RM100,000 as remuneration: Company saves RM24,000 (24% deduction), Director pays RM13,000 tax = NET benefit RM11,000
- RM100,000 as dividend: Company pays RM24,000 tax first, Director receives RM100,000 tax-free = NET cost RM24,000
- Remuneration is BETTER by RM11,000

**WARNING:** Excessive directors' remuneration to related-party directors WILL be challenged under Section 140A (TP rules) and disallowed under Section 39 if not commensurate with services rendered.

## STAGE 4: SUPER-PROFITABLE COMPANIES (Chargeable Income > RM5m)

### 4.1 Incentive Stacking

**Pioneer Status (PS) / Investment Tax Allowance (ITA):**
- PS: Tax exemption on 70-100% of statutory income for 5-10 years
- ITA: 60-100% of QCE utilised against 70-100% of SI for 5 years
- Cannot claim PS and ITA simultaneously for the same activity
- CAN claim PS/ITA for a NEW promoted activity while existing business is taxed normally

**Reinvestment Allowance (Schedule 7A):**
- 60% of QCE against 70% of SI (standard)
- 15 consecutive years from first claim
- For expansion, modernisation, automation, diversification of EXISTING manufacturing/agriculture
- Companies that have exhausted 15-year RA: can apply for NIMP 2030 post-RA incentives (Tier 1: 100% QCE/100% SI for 5 years; Tier 2: 60% QCE/70% SI for 5 years)

**Stacking strategy:**
```
EXISTING BUSINESS (manufacturing):
  → Claim RA on reinvestment capex (60% QCE, 70% SI) for 15 years
  → After 15 years: apply for NIMP 2030 post-RA incentive

NEW ACTIVITY (promoted activity or product):
  → Apply for PS (100% SI exemption for 5+5 years) or ITA (100% QCE/100% SI)
  → Ring-fenced: new activity's income assessed separately during incentive period
  → Post-incentive: income merged with existing business

AUTOMATION/IR 4.0 EQUIPMENT:
  → 200% ACA (not mutually exclusive with RA — can claim on DIFFERENT assets)
  → Budget 2026 ACA window: accelerated write-off on qualifying capex
```

### 4.2 JS-SEZ Opportunities

**Johor-Singapore Special Economic Zone (established January 2025):**

For super-profitable companies with operations that can be located in Johor:
- **CIT rate of 5%** for up to 15-20 years (depending on flagship zone)
- Applications open until 31 December 2034
- Minimum investment and employment commitments required

**Key flagship zones and incentives:**

| Zone | Sector | Incentive |
|------|--------|-----------|
| Forest City SFZ | GSH, Family Offices, Fintech | CIT 0-5% for up to 20 years |
| Kulai-Sedenak | AI, Quantum, Medical, Pharma | CIT 5% for 10-15 years |
| Senai-Skudai | Aerospace/MRO | CIT 5% for 10-15 years |
| Tanjung Pelepas | Smart Logistics | ITA 100% QCE/100% SI for 5 years |

**Strategy:** For companies with genuine ability to relocate or establish new operations in Johor, the 5% rate represents a 79% reduction from the standard 24%. Even partial relocation of qualifying activities can yield massive tax savings.

**Substance requirements:** Must have adequate employees, premises, and decision-making in the zone. Shell operations will be challenged.

### 4.3 Green Technology — GITA

**Green Investment Tax Allowance (applications by 31 December 2026):**

| Tier | Allowance | Against | Qualifying Activities |
|------|-----------|---------|----------------------|
| Tier 1 (assets) | 100% QCE | 70% SI | Battery storage, green buildings |
| Tier 2 (assets) | 60% QCE | 70% SI | Renewable energy, energy efficiency |
| Tier 1 (project) | 100% QCE | 100% or 70% SI | Green hydrogen (10 years) |
| Tier 2 (project) | 100% QCE | 100% SI | Waste management, EV charging (5 years) |
| Tier 3 (project) | 100% QCE | 70% SI | Biomass, biogas, solar, wind (5 years) |

**Budget 2026 addition:** MyHIJAU GITA — 100% GITA for companies using locally-manufactured, MyHIJAU-certified green technology products.

**Strategy for super-profitable companies:**
- Install solar panels, battery storage, EV chargers, energy-efficient equipment
- Apply for GITA BEFORE purchasing/installing (approval must precede expenditure)
- The 100% QCE against 70-100% SI effectively shelters significant income
- Combine with Budget 2026 ACA window for ICT/automation equipment

### 4.4 Labuan Structuring

**Labuan Business Activity Tax Act 1990:**
- Trading activity: **3% of net audited profit** (or election for RM20,000 flat)
- Non-trading activity: **0% tax** (since amendment — previously exempt)
- Must meet substance requirements (gazetted 2019):
  - Minimum 2 full-time employees in Labuan
  - Minimum annual operating expenditure of RM50,000 in Labuan
  - Transactions with residents or non-residents outside of Labuan

**Section 3B, ITA 1967:** Tax under the ITA is NOT charged on income from an offshore business activity carried on by an offshore company that has NOT elected under s.3A of the LBATA.

**When Labuan works (legally):**
- Genuine trading operations with non-Malaysian customers
- Holding company for regional investments
- Treasury/financing centre with real substance
- IP holding (royalty flows through Labuan entity at 3%)

**When Labuan does NOT work / is risky:**
- Transactions primarily with Malaysian entities (substance-over-form challenge)
- Sham operations with no genuine Labuan presence
- Post-2019 substance requirements not met
- LHDN is ACTIVELY targeting Labuan structures under Section 140 where:
  - Management decisions are made in KL/other states, not Labuan
  - Employees in Labuan are nominal/administrative only
  - The Labuan entity is interposed solely for tax benefit

### 4.5 International Structuring

**Regional headquarters incentives:**
- GSH (Global Services Hub): CIT 5-10% for up to 10 years
- Enhanced GSH within JS-SEZ: CIT 5% for up to 15-20 years
- Requires genuine provision of qualifying services to related companies in ≥3 countries

**Holding company structures:**
- Malaysia has a territorial + FSI regime (from YA 2022)
- Foreign-sourced dividends: EXEMPT for resident companies and LLPs (extended to 31 Dec 2030)
- Capital gains on disposal of foreign shares: generally EXEMPT (no CGT on shares — but note RPGT applies to Malaysian real property)
- Withholding tax on dividends paid OUT of Malaysia: 0% (no WHT on dividends under ITA)
- This makes Malaysia attractive as a regional holding company jurisdiction

**WHT optimisation on royalties/technical fees:**
- Domestic WHT rate: 10% (royalties), 10% (technical fees under s.4A)
- Many DTAs reduce to 5-8%
- Structure IP ownership in jurisdictions with favourable DTA rates
- Ensure DTA benefits are not denied under limitation of benefits (LOB) clauses

### 4.6 Investment Holding Company Pitfalls (Section 60F)

**The trap:** If a company's activities consist MAINLY of holding investments and ≥80% of gross income is from investments:
- Each source taxed SEPARATELY (no inter-source offset)
- Expenses apportioned across sources
- Unabsorbed expenses CANNOT be carried forward
- No group relief, no SME rate, no double deductions
- Capital allowances limited to rental assets only

**Avoidance strategies:**
- Ensure the holding company performs GENUINE management/administrative services (charge management fees to subsidiaries — at arm's length)
- Maintain that investment income is < 80% of total gross income (even small service income can prevent 60F)
- Consider whether the holding company should be an IHC listed on Bursa (Section 60FA has slightly different treatment)
- Structure: separate operating activities into the holding company (even partial) to avoid the pure-IHC characterisation

### 4.7 Controlled Transfer Elections (Para 38-40, Schedule 3)

**What it allows:** Tax-neutral transfers of assets between related companies (≥50% common control).

**How it works:**
- Transferor: deemed to dispose at TAX WRITTEN DOWN VALUE (no balancing charge)
- Transferee: deemed cost = transferor's TWDV (no initial allowance; AA continues at same rate)
- Both parties must jointly elect Para 38 treatment

**Strategic uses:**
- Moving high-value assets from one group company to another for commercial restructuring WITHOUT triggering tax
- Pre-sale clean-up: move assets you want to retain to another group company before selling the operating company
- Consolidating assets in the entity best positioned to claim CA

**Clawback (Para 40):** If within 2 years the control relationship ceases or the transferee disposes to a non-connected person, the transfer is UNWOUND retrospectively.

**Planning point:** Time controlled transfers at least 2 years before any planned sale/restructuring that would break the control relationship.

### 4.8 IP Holding Structures

**Strategy:** Centralise intellectual property in a dedicated entity that receives royalties from operating companies.

**Malaysia-specific considerations:**
- Royalties paid to Malaysian-resident companies: NOT subject to WHT (WHT only on payments to non-residents)
- Operating company: deducts royalty payment (reduces income at 24%)
- IP holding entity: receives royalty as income BUT may have:
  - Relevant CA on the IP acquisition cost
  - Incentives (if qualifying technology commercialisation)
  - Lower effective rate if structured under an incentive scheme
- WHT on outbound royalties to non-residents: 10% (or DTA rate if lower)

**Risks:**
- Transfer pricing: royalty rate must be arm's length (benchmark against independent licence rates)
- Anti-avoidance: LHDN may challenge if IP was developed by the operating company and transferred to IP HoldCo without adequate consideration
- Section 140: if the arrangement has no commercial substance beyond tax reduction, LHDN can disregard it

## ANTI-AVOIDANCE RULES — THE BOUNDARIES

### Section 140 — General Anti-Avoidance Provision

**Text (paraphrased from ITA 1967, Chapter 2):**
> The Director General may disregard or vary any transaction where he has reason to believe it has the direct or indirect effect of:
> (a) altering the incidence of tax;
> (b) relieving any person from liability to pay tax or reducing the amount;
> (c) evading or avoiding any duty or liability imposed by the ITA

**Key aspects:**
- Extremely broad — DG needs only "reason to believe"
- No requirement for LHDN to prove intent — effect alone is sufficient
- The DG may counteract the tax advantage by making adjustments as deemed appropriate
- No specific "commercial purpose" defence in the statute (unlike UK/Australia GAAR)
- However, in practice and case law, genuine commercial transactions with incidental tax benefits are generally not attacked

**What triggers Section 140 (based on LHDN audit practice):**
1. Transactions between related parties with no commercial purpose
2. Circular flows of funds (round-tripping)
3. Back-to-back arrangements that only make sense for tax reasons
4. Artificial interposition of entities
5. Transactions priced significantly off-market
6. Structures that separate legal ownership from economic benefit without reason

### Section 140A — Transfer Pricing

**Scope:** Applies to ALL controlled transactions between associated persons (domestic AND cross-border).

**The arm's length principle:** Controlled transactions must reflect pricing that independent parties would agree.

**Penalties for TP adjustments:**
- Primary adjustment: additional tax on the adjusted amount
- Surcharge: 5% penalty on the adjustment amount (separate from late payment penalty)
- No corresponding adjustment given to the counterparty (potential double taxation within the group)

**Documentation requirements:**
- Contemporaneous TP documentation mandatory
- Must contain: industry analysis, functional analysis, comparable analysis, selection of method, economic analysis
- Penalty for non-compliance: fine of RM20,000-RM100,000 and/or imprisonment

### Section 140C — Earnings Stripping Rule

**Effective from:** Rules gazetted (implementation phased)

**What it does:** Restricts deductibility of interest paid to related parties (and in some cases, third-party interest that is guaranteed by related parties).

**Key parameters (per the Income Tax (Restriction on Deductibility of Interest) Rules):**
- Net interest deduction limited to a percentage of tax-EBITDA
- The threshold/percentage is prescribed by rules (currently 20% of tax-EBITDA for financial assistance from related parties)
- Exemptions: banking/insurance companies, certain infrastructure projects
- Excess interest: may be carried forward (subject to conditions)

**Impact on highly-leveraged structures:**
- Companies relying on related-party debt for financing will have interest deductions CAPPED
- This directly counters the traditional tax planning strategy of loading debt onto Malaysian operations
- Restructuring response: convert related-party debt to equity (dividends paid out are not subject to s.140C — but dividends are not deductible either)

### The "Commercial Substance" Test

**When does LHDN disregard a structure?**

LHDN applies a substance-over-form approach in practice. Key indicators that a structure LACKS substance:

| Indicator | Example |
|-----------|---------|
| No genuine employees | Shell company in Labuan with no real staff |
| No real decision-making | Board meetings minutes fabricated or pro-forma |
| No genuine business purpose | Entity exists only to claim tax benefit |
| Artificial pricing | Management fees charged at inflated rates |
| Circular cashflows | Money moves in a circle back to originator |
| Pre-ordained steps | Transaction only makes sense as part of a tax scheme |
| Inconsistent with economic reality | Loss-making entity charges premium prices |

**What PROTECTS a structure:**
- Genuine commercial rationale documented BEFORE implementation
- Real employees performing real functions
- Arms-length pricing with TP documentation
- Board minutes showing decisions based on business factors (not just tax)
- Actual business operations and customer interactions
- Risk borne by the entity matches its reward

## LHDN AUDIT RED FLAGS — BY COMPANY STAGE

### Loss-Making Companies
| Red Flag | Why It Triggers Audit |
|----------|----------------------|
| Perpetual losses (>3 consecutive years) | LHDN suspects understated income or overstated expenses |
| Related-party charges creating/increasing losses | Suspected profit shifting via TP |
| No income but claiming capital allowances | Questions about genuine business activity |
| Large intercompany management fees from profitable related parties | Potential reverse TP — deliberate loss creation |
| Shareholder change immediately before loss utilisation | Suspected loss-buying arrangement |
| Dormant company suddenly showing activity only to utilise losses | Perceived abuse |

### Breakeven Companies
| Red Flag | Why It Triggers Audit |
|----------|----------------------|
| Consistently at breakeven (never paying tax) | Perceived engineering of nil tax position |
| Volatile income/expense patterns around year-end | Suspected timing manipulation |
| Large year-end accruals reversed in subsequent period | Possible artificial expense acceleration |
| Frequent FYE changes | Suspected basis period manipulation |
| Mismatched accounting profit vs tax computation | Large permanent/timing differences unexplained |

### Profitable Companies
| Red Flag | Why It Triggers Audit |
|----------|----------------------|
| Sudden drop in profitability after related-party charges introduced | TP issue |
| Gross profit margin significantly below industry average | Possible income suppression |
| Large claims for double deductions without proper approval letters | Documentation gaps |
| Directors' remuneration disproportionate to company size | Possible profit extraction |
| Multiple companies with identical shareholders all at RM600k chargeable income | Perceived rate-band splitting |
| Entertainment expenses > 5% of turnover | Industry-specific scrutiny |
| Large donations approaching 10% cap every year | Potential circular arrangement |

### Super-Profitable Companies
| Red Flag | Why It Triggers Audit |
|----------|----------------------|
| Significant royalty/technical fee payments to non-residents | WHT compliance + TP |
| Labuan structures with transactions primarily involving Malaysian residents | Substance challenge |
| Incentive claims with questionable qualifying activities | MIDA conditions check |

| Effective tax rate < 10% without PS/ITA approval | General anti-avoidance scrutiny |
| IP migration to related entities | Anti-avoidance + TP + substance |

## TIMING ARBITRAGE STRATEGIES (LEGAL)

### Strategy 1: Accelerate Capital Expenditure into ACA Windows
- Budget 2026 window: 11 Oct 2025 - 31 Dec 2026 (full write-off in ~2 years)
- Automation CA: YA 2023 - YA 2027 (200% write-off)
- Pull forward planned capex to maximise accelerated deductions
- This is EXPLICITLY ENCOURAGED by government policy — no anti-avoidance risk

### Strategy 2: Harvest Losses Before Expiry
- Year 9 of a 10-year loss carry forward: ensure sufficient income to absorb remaining losses
- If necessary: accelerate revenue recognition, defer expenses, or reduce CA claims to create/increase income in that year
- LEGAL: choosing when to recognise income (where discretion exists under accounting standards) is not avoidance

### Strategy 3: Bonus vs Dividend Timing
- Declare bonuses before FYE to create deductions in high-income year
- Pay dividends in low-income year (no corporate deduction anyway)
- Switch to dividends permanently once director's personal rate exceeds corporate rate

### Strategy 4: Donation Matching
- In a year with unexpectedly high income: maximise donations up to 10% cap
- In a low-income year: minimise donations (lower cap = lower absolute deduction anyway)
- Multi-year planning: smooth donations across years based on projected aggregate income

### Strategy 5: Renovation Allowance Window Management
- RM300,000 cap per 3-year period — track when the window resets
- Time large renovations to straddle two windows (Phase 1 in Year 3, Phase 2 in new Year 1)
- Tourism operators: RM500,000 window through 31 Dec 2027

### Strategy 6: Inter-Company Settlement Timing
- Related-party invoices: issue/settle BEFORE FYE if the paying company needs the deduction
- Issue/settle AFTER FYE if the receiving company is in a loss position
- CAUTION: Must be at arm's length and must reflect genuine services; timing manipulation alone without substance is aggressive

### Strategy 7: FYE Selection for New Companies
- Manufacturing with startup losses: choose FYE to maximise the period BEFORE profitability starts (longer loss years = more expenses captured in loss years that carry forward)
- Trading company expecting immediate profits: choose FYE that creates a SHORT first period (less income in first YA, more time to plan)
- Seasonal businesses: FYE after peak season (income recognised early in period, time to manage year-end accruals)

## ETHICAL BOUNDARIES — WHAT IS LEGITIMATE VS AGGRESSIVE

### CLEARLY LEGITIMATE (Standard Planning)
- Choosing the most tax-efficient form of remuneration (salary vs dividend)
- Timing capex to fall within ACA/incentive windows
- Claiming all available double deductions with proper approvals
- Structuring operations to maintain SME status (if genuinely separate businesses)
- Electing controlled transfer treatment for group restructuring
- Choosing not to claim CA in loss years (preserving indefinite-life CA)
- Applying for PS/ITA/RA/GITA incentives for qualifying activities
- Legitimate transfer pricing with proper documentation

### GREY AREA (Aggressive but Defensible if Documented)
- Splitting genuinely related businesses into separate companies for SME rates
- FYE changes to create favourable overlap periods
- Related-party service charges that are at arm's length but structured to shift income
- Labuan structures with genuine (but minimal) substance
- Choosing between capitalising and expensing borderline items based on tax outcome
- Back-dating bonus resolutions to FYE (if genuinely decided before FYE)
- Interposing entities in transaction chains where some commercial purpose exists

### CLEARLY AGGRESSIVE / HIGH RISK
- Artificial loss creation through sham transactions
- Round-tripping funds through related parties to create deductions
- Labuan structures with no real substance (nominee employees, mail drop)
- Related-party charges with no underlying service delivery
- Loss-buying (acquiring loss companies solely for their tax losses)
- Fabricating commercial purpose after the fact to justify tax-motivated transactions
- Claiming incentives for activities that do not genuinely meet the qualifying criteria
- Deliberate mis-characterisation of income (capital vs revenue) without basis
- Transfer pricing that ignores the arm's length principle
