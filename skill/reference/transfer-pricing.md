# Transfer Pricing — Section 140A, ITA 1967

**Legislative basis:** Section 140A, 140B, 140C ITA 1967; Income Tax (Transfer Pricing) Rules 2012 [P.U.(A) 132/2012] (amended 2017, 2019, 2023)
**Administrative guidance:** LHDN Transfer Pricing Guidelines 2012
**Key Public Rulings:** PR 1/2012 (Application of Arm's Length Principle)

---

## 1. Scope — Section 140A

### Who It Applies To

The Director General may make adjustments where:
- A person is in a **controlled transaction** with an **associated person**
- The transaction is NOT at arm's length
- A tax advantage is or would be obtained

### "Associated Persons" — Section 140A(5), (5A), Section 139

Two persons are associated if:
- One has **control** over the other; OR
- Both are controlled by a **third person**

**"Control"** (Section 139) means:
- Holding >50% of paid-up share capital; OR
- Power to appoint majority of board of directors; OR
- Power to govern the financial and operating policies

**Expanded definition (s.140A(5A)):**
- Two persons in a business relationship where one is financially dependent on the other
- Person and permanent establishment
- Two PEs of the same person

**"Relative"** for individual associated persons: spouse, parent, child, brother, sister, grandparent, uncle, aunt, nephew, niece.

### Applies to BOTH Cross-Border AND Domestic

Section 140A does NOT limit itself to international transactions. It applies to transactions between associated persons whether in Malaysia or abroad. LHDN increasingly audits domestic intra-group pricing.

## 2. The Arm's Length Principle

The price or margin in a controlled transaction must be consistent with what would have been agreed between **independent parties** dealing at arm's length in comparable circumstances.

---

## 3. Transfer Pricing Methods

### Method Selection Hierarchy

The TP Rules require the **most appropriate method** to be used. There is no strict hierarchy, but CUP is preferred where reliable comparables exist.

### Method 1: Comparable Uncontrolled Price (CUP)

```
WHAT: Compares price charged in controlled transaction to price charged
      in comparable uncontrolled transaction.

WHEN TO USE:
  - Identical or similar product/service sold to independent parties
  - Reliable comparable transactions available
  - Best method for commodity/standard product transactions

FORMULA: Transfer price = comparable uncontrolled price (adjusted for differences)
```

### Method 2: Resale Price Method (RPM)

```
WHAT: Starts with resale price to independent party, deducts appropriate
      gross margin to arrive at arm's length transfer price.

WHEN TO USE:
  - Distributor buys from related manufacturer, resells to independents
  - Distributor adds limited value (no significant transformation)
  - Reliable comparable resale margins available

FORMULA: Arm's length price = Resale price × (1 - gross margin %)
```

### Method 3: Cost Plus Method (CPM)

```
WHAT: Starts with costs incurred by supplier in controlled transaction,
      adds appropriate markup to arrive at arm's length price.

WHEN TO USE:
  - Contract manufacturing / toll manufacturing
  - Intra-group services (management, technical, support)
  - Semi-finished goods between related manufacturers

FORMULA: Arm's length price = Costs + (Costs × markup %)

SAFE HARBOUR: 5% cost-plus for LOW-VALUE intra-group services
              (routine, non-core, not significant to business)
```

### Method 4: Transactional Net Margin Method (TNMM)

```
WHAT: Compares net profit margin relative to an appropriate base (costs,
      sales, assets) that a taxpayer realises in a controlled transaction
      to that of comparable independent transactions.

WHEN TO USE:
  - Most commonly used method globally and in Malaysia
  - Where reliable gross margin comparables not available
  - For complex transactions involving multiple functions
  - One party is the "tested party" (less complex entity)

FORMULA: Compare net margin indicator (e.g., operating profit/sales)
         against comparable company benchmarks (interquartile range)

COMMON PLIs (Profit Level Indicators):
  - Operating margin (EBIT/revenue)
  - Full cost markup (EBIT/total costs)
  - Berry ratio (gross profit/operating expenses)
  - Return on assets (EBIT/total assets)
```

### Method 5: Profit Split Method (PSM)

```
WHAT: Splits combined profit from controlled transaction between associated
      persons based on relative value of each party's contribution.

WHEN TO USE:
  - Both parties make unique, valuable contributions
  - Highly integrated operations
  - No reliable one-sided comparable exists
  - Unique intangibles on both sides

TWO APPROACHES:
  (a) Contribution analysis — split based on relative value of functions/assets/risks
  (b) Residual analysis — allocate routine returns first, split residual profit
```

## 4. Documentation Requirements

### Thresholds — Who MUST Prepare TP Documentation

| Threshold | Requirement |
|-----------|-------------|
| Gross income > RM25 million AND related party transactions > RM15 million | MANDATORY full TP documentation |
| Financial assistance (loans) > RM50 million with related parties | MANDATORY TP documentation for that transaction |
| Below thresholds | Simplified records acceptable; but arm's length still required |

### Contents of TP Documentation

```
MANDATORY CONTENTS:
1. Organisational structure (group chart, ownership)
2. Nature of business / industry analysis
3. Controlled transactions:
   - Type (goods, services, interest, royalties, etc.)
   - Amount / quantum
   - Terms and conditions
   - Parties involved
4. Functional analysis:
   - Functions performed by each party
   - Assets employed
   - Risks assumed
5. Economic analysis:
   - Comparability analysis
   - Selection of TP method (with justification)
   - Application of method
   - Comparable data / benchmarking study
   - Financial data used
6. Conclusion — arm's length range/result
7. Supporting evidence (contracts, invoices, correspondence)
```

### Timing — Contemporaneous Requirement

- Documentation must be prepared **at the time of the transaction** or by the **tax return filing deadline** for the relevant YA
- Must be available for LHDN inspection **within 14 days** of request during audit
- Backdated documentation (prepared only when audited) is NOT contemporaneous and may not be accepted

### Penalties for Non-Compliance

| Offence | Penalty |
|---------|---------|
| No TP documentation (or inadequate) | 5% surcharge on any TP adjustment made |
| Incorrect return due to non-arm's length pricing | Section 113: penalty up to 100% of tax undercharged |
| Failure to provide documentation within 14 days | Prosecution: RM200-RM20,000 fine or ≤ 6 months jail |

## 5. The 5% Surcharge

### Mechanics (Section 140A(3))

```
IF LHDN makes a TP adjustment (increases taxable income):
  → Additional tax on the adjustment is charged at normal rate (24%)
  → PLUS a surcharge of 5% on the TP adjustment amount

CALCULATION:
  TP Adjustment amount:                           RM 1,000,000
  Additional tax (24%):                           RM   240,000
  Surcharge (5% of RM1,000,000):                  RM    50,000
  Section 113 penalty (up to 100% of RM240,000):  RM   240,000
                                                  ────────────
  TOTAL EXPOSURE:                                 RM   530,000

The surcharge is IN ADDITION to any Section 113 penalty.
```

### Avoiding the Surcharge

- Maintain contemporaneous TP documentation → surcharge does NOT apply even if adjustment is made (provided documentation meets requirements)
- Apply for APA → agreed pricing eliminates adjustment risk

## 6. Advance Pricing Arrangement (APA) — Section 138C

### Types

| Type | Parties | Scope |
|------|---------|-------|
| Unilateral | Taxpayer + LHDN only | Malaysia only |
| Bilateral | Taxpayer + LHDN + foreign tax authority | Both countries |
| Multilateral | Taxpayer + LHDN + multiple foreign authorities | All countries |

### Process

```
1. PRE-FILING CONSULTATION (optional but recommended)
   - Informal discussion with LHDN
   - Assess feasibility of APA

2. FORMAL APPLICATION
   - Submit to LHDN APA Unit
   - Include: proposed methodology, comparables, projected results, financial info
   - Fee payable on application

3. EVALUATION AND NEGOTIATION
   - LHDN reviews application
   - May request additional information
   - For bilateral: competent authority negotiations with treaty partner

4. APA AGREEMENT
   - Terms agreed: methodology, arm's length range, critical assumptions
   - Typical coverage: 3-5 years prospective
   - Rollback possible (apply agreed methodology to prior years)

5. COMPLIANCE REPORTING
   - Annual compliance report showing actual results vs. APA terms
   - Must remain within agreed range
```

### Fees

| Type | Fee (RM) |
|------|----------|
| Unilateral APA | RM50,000 |
| Bilateral/Multilateral APA | RM100,000 |

### Timeline
- Unilateral: 12-24 months from application to agreement
- Bilateral: 24-48 months (depends on treaty partner responsiveness)

### Critical Assumptions
APA contains "critical assumptions" — if business conditions change materially (market collapse, new competitor, restructuring), the APA may need to be revised or may become inapplicable.

## 7. Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR)

### Threshold
- Malaysian MNE groups with **consolidated group revenue ≥ RM3 billion**
- OR: Malaysian constituent entity of a foreign MNE group above this threshold (surrogate filing)

### What to File (3 Tables)

```
TABLE 1: Overview of allocation of income, taxes, and business activities
         by tax jurisdiction (revenue, profit/loss, tax paid/accrued,
         stated capital, accumulated earnings, employees, tangible assets)

TABLE 2: List of all constituent entities of the MNE group, grouped by
         tax jurisdiction, with business activities described

TABLE 3: Additional explanatory information
```

### Deadline
- Within **12 months** after the close of the reporting fiscal year
- Filed electronically via MyTax portal

### Penalties
- Failure to file CbCR: prosecution under Section 120 (RM200-RM20,000 fine or ≤ 6 months jail)
- Inaccurate CbCR: penalties under Section 119A

## 8. Earnings Stripping Rules — Section 140C

### Purpose
Replaces thin capitalisation rules. Limits deductibility of interest on related-party financial assistance.

### Scope
Applies to **interest expense** on **financial assistance** in a **controlled transaction** that exceeds the prescribed threshold.

### Key Parameters

| Parameter | Rule |
|-----------|------|
| De minimis threshold | RM500,000 — if total interest in controlled transactions ≤ RM500,000, Section 140C does NOT apply |
| Maximum deduction | **20% of tax-EBITDA** from each business source |
| Tax-EBITDA | Adjusted income + interest expense + capital allowances (i.e., earnings before interest, tax, depreciation, amortisation for tax purposes) |
| Carry forward | Excess interest (disallowed) can be carried forward **indefinitely** |
| Carry forward condition | Subject to shareholders' continuity test (>50% change = lost) |

### What Counts as "Financial Assistance"
- Loans (interest-bearing)
- Advances
- Hire purchase financing
- Any form of credit or debt arrangement

### Exclusions
- Financial institutions (banks, insurance companies)
- Companies in specified industries (infrastructure, public utilities — check gazette)
- Interest ≤ RM500,000 threshold

### Domestic vs Cross-Border
- Statutory text is broad (applies to controlled transactions without geographic limit)
- In practice: primarily targets cross-border arrangements and domestic arrangements linked to cross-border funding
- Purely domestic interest between unrelated parties: NOT caught

### Calculation Example

```
Company has:
  Adjusted income (before interest):          RM 5,000,000
  Interest expense (controlled transaction):  RM 2,000,000
  Capital allowances:                         RM   500,000

  Tax-EBITDA = 5,000,000 + 2,000,000 + 500,000 = RM 7,500,000
  20% of tax-EBITDA = RM 1,500,000

  Interest claimed: RM 2,000,000
  Maximum allowed:  RM 1,500,000
  DISALLOWED:       RM   500,000 (carried forward)
```

## 9. Common Controlled Transactions

### Management Fees

```
RISK LEVEL: HIGH (most commonly challenged by LHDN)

REQUIREMENTS:
□ Written service agreement (signed before services commence)
□ Description of specific services to be provided
□ Evidence services were ACTUALLY performed (deliverables, reports, time records)
□ Benefit test: recipient ACTUALLY benefited (not just shareholder activities)
□ No duplication with services already available internally
□ Arm's length fee (benchmark against comparable independent arrangements)
□ Acceptable methods: CUP (if comparable), Cost Plus (5-10% markup typical)

COMMON LHDN CHALLENGES:
- "What did you actually receive for RM500k management fee?"
- "Isn't this just the parent protecting its investment?" (shareholder activity)
- "Your staff already do this work" (duplication)
- "Show me the time sheets and deliverables" (evidence)
```

### Royalties

```
RISK LEVEL: HIGH

REQUIREMENTS:
□ Licence/royalty agreement
□ Identify the IP being licensed (trademark, patent, know-how, copyright)
□ Demonstrate the IP has VALUE to the licensee's business
□ Arm's length royalty rate (benchmark against comparable licence agreements)
□ Relief from Double Taxation (WHT + TP must both be addressed)
□ WHT compliance: 10% deducted and remitted to LHDN

COMMON RATES (arm's length ranges):
- Trademarks/brand: 1-5% of net sales
- Technology/know-how: 3-8% of net sales
- Patents: varies widely by industry
- Combined (trademark + technology): 5-10%
```

### Inter-Company Loans (Interest)

```
RISK LEVEL: MEDIUM-HIGH

REQUIREMENTS:
□ Written loan agreement (amount, tenure, interest rate, repayment schedule)
□ Interest rate at arm's length (benchmark against bank lending rates)
□ Demonstrate the borrower NEEDED the loan (not just circular funding)
□ Earnings stripping check (Section 140C — is interest > RM500k? If yes, 20% tax-EBITDA cap)
□ WHT if lender is non-resident: 15% (or treaty rate)

BENCHMARKING APPROACHES:
- Bank quotation for comparable loan
- Published lending rates (BNM, bank published rates)
- Comparable loan transactions in market
- Credit rating analysis
```

### Sale/Purchase of Goods

```
RISK LEVEL: MEDIUM

REQUIREMENTS:
□ CUP preferred (if same goods sold to independents)
□ If no CUP: Resale Price (distributor) or Cost Plus (manufacturer)
□ Adjustments for volume, terms, functions, risks, market conditions
□ Customs value must be consistent with transfer price
□ Check for "toll manufacturing" arrangements (limited risk entity)

LHDN FOCUS:
- Goods sold to related distributor at below-market price (profit shifted out)
- Goods purchased from related manufacturer at inflated price
- Inconsistency between customs declared value and income tax price
```

### Service Fees (Technical, Consulting)

```
RISK LEVEL: MEDIUM-HIGH

SAME REQUIREMENTS AS MANAGEMENT FEES, PLUS:
□ Technical content must be genuine (not relabelled management fee)
□ WHT applies to technical fees paid to non-residents (10% under s.4A/109B)
□ If WHT not deducted → expense DISALLOWED (Section 39(2))
□ DTA may exempt or reduce WHT for technical services

SAFE HARBOUR: 5% cost-plus markup for "low-value intra-group services"
(routine, non-core, supporting services that are not the main business activity)
```

### Cost Sharing / Recharges

```
RISK LEVEL: MEDIUM

REQUIREMENTS:
□ Cost sharing agreement (CSA) or cost contribution arrangement (CCA)
□ Each participant's share based on expected benefits
□ No markup on pure recharges (pass-through at cost)
□ Markup required if services/value added beyond pure cost pass-through
□ Must demonstrate reasonable allocation keys (headcount, revenue, etc.)
□ Review annually — allocation must reflect actual benefits received
```

## 10. Mutual Agreement Procedure (MAP)

### When to Use
- When a TP adjustment in Malaysia would result in **double taxation** (same income taxed in two countries)
- Taxpayer applies to the competent authority of EITHER or BOTH countries
- Available only where a DTA exists between Malaysia and the other country

### Process
```
1. Taxpayer files MAP application with LHDN (within time limit per DTA, usually 3 years)
2. LHDN competent authority contacts treaty partner competent authority
3. Negotiations between competent authorities
4. If agreement reached: both countries adjust accordingly
5. If no agreement: double taxation persists (taxpayer may pursue arbitration if DTA provides)
```

### Timeline
- Typically 24-36 months
- Malaysia committed to resolving MAP cases within 24 months under BEPS Action 14

### Interaction with Domestic Appeals
- MAP can run simultaneously with domestic appeal (Form Q to SCIT)
- If MAP resolves the issue, domestic appeal may be withdrawn
- If domestic appeal resolves first, MAP may become moot

## 11. LHDN TP Audit Approach

### Risk Indicators LHDN Looks For

```
□ Large related party transactions relative to total transactions
□ Consistent losses despite group profitability
□ Significant payments to low-tax jurisdictions
□ Royalties/management fees to parent (especially if newly introduced)
□ Year-on-year margin fluctuations without commercial explanation
□ Transactions with Labuan entities
□ Benchmarking results showing taxpayer below arm's length range
□ No TP documentation available
□ Inconsistency with CbCR data
□ Change in functional profile (e.g., full-fledged to limited risk entity)
```

### Common Adjustments Made by LHDN

```
1. Management fees disallowed entirely (no evidence of benefit)
2. Royalties reduced (rate exceeds comparable benchmarks)
3. Interest rate adjusted to market rate (below-market loans)
4. Markup applied to services recharged at cost (LHDN argues value-added)
5. Sales price increased (goods sold below comparable range)
6. Entire transaction recharacterised (e.g., loan → equity contribution)
```

### Defence Strategies

```
□ Maintain contemporaneous TP documentation (avoids 5% surcharge)
□ Conduct benchmarking study annually (refresh comparables)
□ Ensure all intercompany agreements are in writing and signed
□ Keep evidence of services actually performed (reports, emails, deliverables)
□ Review profit margins annually against benchmarks
□ Consider APA for recurring large transactions
□ Ensure consistency across different filings (Form C, customs, SST)
```

## 12. Corresponding Adjustments

### What It Is
When LHDN adjusts Company A's income upward (TP adjustment), the associated person (Company B) has effectively been "double-taxed" on the same income. A corresponding adjustment in the other country would reduce Company B's income to eliminate double taxation.

### Malaysia's Position
- Malaysia will provide corresponding adjustments under DTAs (Article 9(2) equivalent)
- For **domestic** transactions: both parties can amend their returns
- For **cross-border**: must go through MAP to get the other country to adjust

### Practical Reality
- Getting a corresponding adjustment from the other country can be very difficult
- If the other country does not adjust, the group suffers permanent double taxation
- This is why APAs and proper documentation are critical prevention tools
