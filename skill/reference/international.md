# International Taxation — Malaysia

**Authority:** Income Tax Act 1967 (Sections 2, 3, 7, 12, 15A, 21A, 107A, 109, 109B, 109F, 132, 133); Finance Act 2022 (FSI amendments); Labuan Business Activity Tax Act 1990 (LBATA); LHDN Public Rulings  
**Effective:** YA 2025/2026 (with Budget 2026 changes)

---

## 1. RESIDENCE AND SOURCE RULES

### 1.1 Tax Residence — Companies

A company is **resident** in Malaysia if its management and control is exercised in Malaysia at any time during the basis period [s.2(1)].

**Management and control** = where directors meet and make strategic decisions:
- Board meetings held in Malaysia → resident
- Board meetings held overseas → non-resident (even if incorporated in Malaysia)
- Mixed locations → depends on where key decisions are taken (LHDN scrutinises)

**Impact of non-residence:**
- No access to SME rate, tax incentives (PS/ITA), group relief, or treaty benefits
- No DTA relief (Malaysia cannot be "resident state")
- Higher WHT on payments received from Malaysia

### 1.2 Tax Residence — Individuals

Resident if present in Malaysia ≥182 days in a calendar year [s.7(1)(a)], or other connecting tests under s.7(1)(b)-(d).

### 1.3 Source Rules — Where Is Income Derived?

Malaysia taxes income **accruing in or derived from** Malaysia [s.3(1)]:

| Income Type | Source in Malaysia If... |
|---|---|
| Business | Operations/activities carried out in Malaysia [s.12(1)(a)] |
| Employment | Services exercised in Malaysia [s.13(2)] |
| Dividends | Paid by a resident company [s.12(1)(b)] |
| Interest | Paid by a resident or charged on property in Malaysia [s.12(1)(c)] |
| Royalties | Paid by a resident or used in Malaysia [s.12(1)(d)] |
| Rental (movables) | Property used in Malaysia [s.12(1)(e)] |
| Services (technical/management) | Services performed in or borne by Malaysian resident [s.15A] |

### 1.4 Foreign-Sourced Income (FSI) — From YA 2022

**Key change:** Finance Act 2022 abolished the FSI exemption. Foreign-sourced income **received in Malaysia** by a resident is NOW TAXABLE.

**"Received in Malaysia"** means:
- Remitted/transmitted/brought into Malaysia
- Used to settle Malaysian debts
- Used to buy property/assets in Malaysia
- Constructive remittance (benefit enjoyed in Malaysia funded by foreign income)

**FSI Exemptions still available (gazette conditions apply):**
- Individuals: ALL FSI exempt (from YA 2022, indefinitely extended in Budget 2023)
- Companies: FSI dividend income exempt if underlying tax paid in source country ≥15%
- Companies/LLPs: certain categories exempt until 31 Dec 2026 (as extended by Budget 2025):
  - Interest, rental, royalties, services income — IF tax paid at ≥15% in source country
  - Capital gains from disposal of overseas assets (subject to conditions)

**NOT exempt (always taxable on remittance):**
- Business profits from foreign PE (unless treaty relief)
- Foreign partnership income allocated to Malaysian resident partner
- FSI where NO underlying tax paid in source country

### 1.5 Permanent Establishment (PE)

A PE exists in Malaysia if there is a **fixed place of business** through which business is wholly or partly carried on [s.12(3) + DTA Article 5]:

**Creates PE:**
- Branch office, factory, workshop, warehouse
- Building/construction site > 6 months (or 12 months under some DTAs)
- Dependent agent with authority to conclude contracts
- Installation/assembly project > 6 months
- Services furnished for > 183 days in any 12-month period (under newer DTAs)

**Does NOT create PE:**
- Storage/display of goods
- Purchasing office only
- Preparatory/auxiliary activities
- Independent agent (broker, commission agent)

**Tax consequence of PE:**
- PE = taxable presence → file Form C, assessed on Malaysian-source profits
- 24% corporate rate (no SME rate for non-resident)
- Branch profits (after-tax) → subject to branch profit remittance [not currently imposed in Malaysia, but tracked]

## 2. DOUBLE TAXATION AGREEMENTS (DTAs)

### 2.1 Malaysia's Treaty Network

Malaysia has **76+ DTAs** in force (as at 2025). Key partners: Singapore, UK, Australia, Japan, China, India, Thailand, Indonesia, UAE, Netherlands, Luxembourg, Germany, USA (limited agreement).

### 2.2 DTA Reduced WHT Rates (Selected Major Partners)

| Country | Interest | Royalties | Technical Fees |
|---|---|---|---|
| Singapore | 10% | 8% | 5% |
| UK | 10% | 8% | exempt |
| Australia | 15% | 10% | 10% |
| Japan | 10% | 10% | 0% |
| China | 10% | 10% | 10% |
| India | 10% | 10% | 10% |
| Thailand | 15% | 10% | treaty silent |
| Indonesia | 10% | 10% | 10% |
| Netherlands | 10% | 8% | 8% |
| UAE | 0% | 8% | 10% |
| Hong Kong | 10% | 8% | 5% |
| South Korea | 15% | 10% | 10% |

*Note: Domestic WHT rates apply if HIGHER than treaty rate is claimed (cannot increase via treaty)*

### 2.3 Applying Treaty Benefits

To claim reduced DTA rates:
1. Non-resident must be **resident** of the treaty country (Certificate of Residence required)
2. Malaysian payer must obtain COR BEFORE payment date
3. Payer remits WHT at treaty rate (not domestic rate)
4. If paid at domestic rate → apply for refund via Section 132 procedure
5. Beneficial ownership must be with the treaty country resident (not conduit/back-to-back)

**Anti-treaty shopping:** LHDN applies beneficial ownership test + principal purpose test (PPT) under MLI (Malaysia ratified MLI, effective from 1 Jan 2021 for DTAs)

### 2.4 Limitation on Benefits

Under Malaysia's MLI positions:
- PPT applies to all covered tax agreements
- No LOB (limitation on benefits) clause adopted
- Simplified LOB for certain DTAs only where specified

## 3. FOREIGN TAX CREDIT (FTC)

### 3.1 Section 132 — Treaty Credit

Where a DTA exists between Malaysia and the source country:
- Tax paid in the source country → credited against Malaysian tax on the SAME income
- Credit limited to: **lower of** (i) foreign tax paid, or (ii) Malaysian tax attributable to that income

**Computation:**
```
Malaysian tax on foreign income = (Foreign income / Total income) × Total Malaysian tax
FTC allowed = LOWER OF:
  (a) Actual foreign tax paid on that income
  (b) Malaysian tax attributable (computed above)
```

### 3.2 Section 133 — Unilateral Credit (No Treaty)

Where NO DTA exists:
- Malaysia grants unilateral relief — 50% of the foreign tax paid is creditable
- Same limitation applies: cannot exceed Malaysian tax on that income
- Computation: credit = **LOWER OF** (i) 50% × foreign tax paid, or (ii) Malaysian tax attributable

### 3.3 FTC Practical Rules

- Credit is computed **per source country** (not blended)
- Excess FTC CANNOT be carried forward or back (lost in the year)
- Documentation: foreign tax assessment/certificate, evidence of payment
- Exchange rate: rate at date of payment of foreign tax
- If underlying tax (dividend from foreign subsidiary): credit for underlying corporate tax IF DTA provides for it

### 3.4 FSI and FTC Interaction

For FSI taxable on remittance (post-YA 2022):
- Foreign tax paid in source country → credit under s.132 (if DTA) or s.133 (no DTA)
- If FSI is EXEMPT (e.g., individual, or meets gazette conditions) → no FTC claim needed (income not taxed)

## 4. WITHHOLDING TAX — OUTBOUND PAYMENTS

### 4.1 Domestic WHT Rates (No Treaty)

| Payment Type | Section | Rate | Due Date |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interest | 109 | 15% | Within 1 month of paying/crediting |
| Royalties | 109 | 10% | Within 1 month |
| Technical/management fees | 109B | 10% | Within 1 month |
| Rental of movables | 109B | 10% | Within 1 month |
| Contract payments (non-resident contractor) | 107A | 10% + 3% = 13% | Within 1 month |
| Special classes (s.4A): installation, services in connection with use of property | 109B | 10% | Within 1 month |
| Public entertainer | 109A | 15% | Within 1 month |
| Interest (approved non-resident bank/financial institution) | 109 | 0% | — |
| Commission/guarantee fees | 109B | 10% | Within 1 month |

### 4.2 Section 107A — Non-Resident Contractor

Applies when contract payment made to non-resident for services under a contract:
- Rate: 10% of contract value (retained as tax of non-resident person) + 3% (employees of non-resident)
- Contract value = total gross payment
- Non-resident may apply to LHDN for exemption/reduced rate (if actual profit is lower)
- If no exemption obtained: full 13% deducted, non-resident files Form M for refund

### 4.3 WHT and Deductibility

**Critical rule [s.39(2)]:** If WHT is required but NOT deducted and remitted to LHDN:
- The expense is **NON-DEDUCTIBLE** until the WHT is actually paid
- Penalty: 10% of WHT due + interest on late remittance

### 4.4 Reverse Charge (Services Tax)

From 1 January 2019, imported services subject to 8% service tax (reverse charge mechanism). This is SEPARATE from income tax WHT — both may apply simultaneously.

## 5. LABUAN ENTITIES

### 5.1 Labuan Business Activity Tax Act 1990 (LBATA)

Labuan entities (Labuan companies, LLPs, trusts, foundations) are taxed under LBATA, NOT the ITA 1967.

**Tax regime:**
| Type | Rate (post-2020 amendments) |
|---|---|
| Labuan trading activity (with substance) | 3% of net profit |
| Labuan non-trading activity | 0% (exempt) |
| Labuan entity electing ITA treatment | 24% (standard corporate rate) |
| Labuan entity failing substance test | 24% (deemed elected ITA) |

### 5.2 Substance Requirements (from YA 2019, strengthened 2025)

A Labuan entity conducting **trading activity** must satisfy ALL:
1. Minimum **2 full-time employees** in Labuan (at least 1 must be Malaysian)
2. Minimum annual **operating expenditure** of RM50,000 in Labuan
3. Activities directed and managed FROM Labuan

**Budget 2025/2026 enhancements:**
- LHDN conducts annual substance audits on Labuan entities
- Penalties for sham substance arrangements strengthened
- Inter-company transactions with Labuan entities → transfer pricing rules apply in full

### 5.3 Interaction with ITA 1967

- Payments from Malaysian company TO Labuan entity:
  - Interest, royalties, technical fees → WHT applies (Labuan entity treated as non-resident for this purpose since 2019 amendment)
  - Service fees → WHT if falls under s.4A/109B
- Payments FROM Labuan entity to non-residents:
  - Not subject to Malaysian WHT (outside ITA jurisdiction)
- Labuan entity making election to be taxed under ITA:
  - Loses Labuan preferential rates permanently (irrevocable from YA 2020)
  - Full ITA compliance required (Form C, CP204, etc.)

### 5.4 EU Blacklisting Context

Malaysia's Labuan regime was on EU's tax haven grey list until reforms were made. Current position:
- Labuan regime is partially compliant with OECD BEPS standards
- Substance requirements were introduced specifically to address EU/OECD concerns
- Multinational groups must factor reputational risk into Labuan structuring decisions

## 6. BRANCH VS SUBSIDIARY

### 6.1 Decision Framework

| Factor | Branch (PE) | Subsidiary (Sdn Bhd) |
|---|---|---|
| Legal status | Extension of foreign parent | Separate legal entity |
| Tax residence | Non-resident (taxed as PE) | Resident (if managed from MY) |
| Tax rate | 24% flat (no SME rate) | 24% (or SME tiered rates) |
| Incentives | Cannot claim PS/ITA/SME rates | Full access to incentives |
| Group relief | Not available | Available (if conditions met) |
| DTA benefits | Limited | Full (as resident) |
| Losses | Offset against parent (in some jurisdictions) | Ring-fenced in Malaysia |
| WHT on profit repatriation | None (no branch profit tax in MY) | None (single-tier dividend) |
| Filing | Form C-BT (branch) | Form C |
| Winding up | Closure notification | Full liquidation process |

### 6.2 When to Choose Branch

- Short-term project (< 3 years)
- Expecting losses (can offset against parent in home country, if their law permits)
- No need for Malaysian incentives
- Simpler operations (no local equity required)

### 6.3 When to Choose Subsidiary

- Long-term presence
- Want SME rates or tax incentives
- Want group relief (between Malaysian companies)
- Want to access DTA benefits as Malaysia-resident
- Need limited liability protection distinct from parent

## 7. CONTROLLED FOREIGN COMPANY (CFC) — MALAYSIA DOES NOT HAVE CFC RULES

**Important:** Malaysia has NO CFC legislation. There is no rule that attributes income of a foreign subsidiary to a Malaysian parent company merely because:
- The subsidiary is in a low-tax jurisdiction
- The subsidiary is controlled by Malaysian shareholders
- The subsidiary accumulates passive income offshore

**Implication:**
- Foreign subsidiary profits are NOT taxed in Malaysia until remitted (as dividends)
- When remitted → FSI rules apply (taxable from YA 2022 unless exempt category)
- No deemed distribution rules
- However: transfer pricing rules (s.140A) still apply to inter-company pricing

## 8. MUTUAL AGREEMENT PROCEDURE (MAP)

Where a taxpayer faces **double taxation** not in accordance with a DTA:
1. Taxpayer applies to competent authority (LHDN International Division) within **3 years** of first notification of the action
2. LHDN engages with treaty partner's authority
3. Resolution may take 12-36 months
4. Outcome may include: allocation of profits between jurisdictions, credit adjustments, reduction of assessment

**Arbitration:** Malaysia has NOT adopted the mandatory binding arbitration provision under the MLI (reserved position).

## 9. COMMON INTERNATIONAL TAX SCENARIOS

### 9.1 Malaysian Company Paying Foreign Service Provider

```
CHECK:
1. Is the payment for services under s.4A (technical, management, consultancy)?
   → YES: WHT 10% [s.109B] (or DTA rate if lower)
   → Ensure WHT remitted within 1 month
   → If not deducted: expense NON-DEDUCTIBLE [s.39(2)]

2. Does the non-resident have a PE in Malaysia?
   → YES: No WHT — non-resident files Form C on PE profits instead
   → NO: WHT applies on gross payment

3. Is there a DTA?
   → YES: Apply treaty rate (obtain COR from non-resident)
   → NO: Apply domestic 10% rate
```

### 9.2 Malaysian Company Receiving Foreign Income

```
CHECK:
1. Income source? (interest, dividend, royalties, business profit, services)
2. Was foreign tax deducted at source?
   → Record amount and obtain foreign tax certificate
3. Is income EXEMPT under FSI gazette conditions?
   → If YES: do not include in Malaysian taxable income
   → If NO: include as Malaysian assessable income
4. Claim FTC:
   → DTA exists: s.132 credit (full foreign tax, limited to MY tax on that income)
   → No DTA: s.133 credit (50% of foreign tax, limited to MY tax on that income)
5. Exchange rate: use BNM rate at date income is remitted/received
```

### 9.3 Non-Resident Earning Malaysian Income

```
CHECK:
1. Nature of income?
   - Business: taxed only if PE in Malaysia
   - Employment: taxed if services exercised in Malaysia
   - Passive (interest/royalties/rent): WHT is final tax
   - Contract payments: 13% WHT [s.107A], may file Form M for actual assessment

2. DTA relief available?
   - Business profits: not taxed if no PE (treaty protection)
   - Services: may be exempt if < 183 days and threshold not met
   - Passive: reduced rates per treaty

3. Filing obligations:
   - PE: Form C (resident rates may not apply)
   - No PE: WHT is final (no filing required unless claiming refund)
```

### 9.4 Outbound Investment Structure

```
Malaysian parent → Foreign subsidiary:
- Dividends received: FSI (taxable on remittance, unless exempt category)
- Management fees charged: Malaysian income (no WHT issue, it's revenue TO Malaysia)
- Loans extended: interest received = Malaysian income IF originated from Malaysia
- Guarantee fees: taxable in Malaysia when received

Malaysian parent → Foreign branch:
- Branch profits: Malaysian income (if parent is resident, worldwide basis pre-2022)
- Post-2022: only taxable when REMITTED (FSI rules)
- Branch losses: can offset against Malaysian income (if same business)
```

## 10. EXCHANGE RATE RULES

- Revenue transactions (trade receivables/payables): use rate at transaction date; forex gains/losses are revenue (taxable/deductible)
- Capital transactions (loans, share investments): forex gains/losses are capital (non-taxable/non-deductible) — UNLESS the loan is trade-related
- Unrealised forex: from YA 2019, unrealised forex on monetary items is taxable/deductible (for trade items) — PR 12/2019
- FSI remittance: use BNM rate at date of remittance for conversion
- WHT: use rate at date payment is made/credited to non-resident

## 11. TAX INFORMATION EXCHANGE

Malaysia participates in:
- **Automatic Exchange of Information (AEOI/CRS)** — effective from September 2018
- **Exchange of Information on Request (EOIR)** — via DTAs and TIEAs
- **Country-by-Country Reporting (CbCR)** — MNE groups with revenue ≥RM3 billion

**Implication for taxpayers:**
- Offshore income/assets are increasingly visible to LHDN
- Failure to declare FSI on remittance → high audit risk
- LHDN accesses CRS data from 100+ jurisdictions
