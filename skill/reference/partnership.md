# Partnerships - Tax Rules (Form P)

**Form:** P
**Legislative basis:** Income Tax Act 1967, Sections 55-59, 86
**Key Public Rulings:** PR 7/2021 (Part I), PR 8/2021 (Part II - Computation & Allocation)
**Filing deadline:** 30 June of the following year
**Filed by:** Precedent partner

---

## 1. Fundamental Principle: Tax Transparency

**Section 86; Sections 55-59**

A partnership is NOT a chargeable person. It is not a separate legal entity. No assessment can be raised on the partnership itself.

Income derived from the partnership is allocated to partners based on the profit sharing ratio and taxed in the hands of each partner individually. Each partner is deemed to be a sole proprietor conducting their own business (Section 55).

Section 55 postulates that:
- The business and assets of the partnership, together with the rights and liabilities of the partners, are deemed to have been transferred to each partner
- The transfer constitutes a business carried on by each partner as a sole proprietor

### Non-Resident Partners (Section 70(2))
Income assessable and chargeable in the name of:
- The partnership; or
- Any partner who is resident for that basis year; or
- Any agent of the partnership in Malaysia

Tax charged is recoverable from partnership assets.

## 3. Divisible vs Non-Divisible Income

**Divisible Income:** Portion of PAI divided among partners per profit sharing ratio after deducting non-divisible items.

**Non-divisible (directly attributable) amounts:**
- Partners' remuneration → allocated to specific partner
- Interest on each partner's capital → allocated to specific partner
- Private expenses of each partner → allocated to specific partner
- Loan interest paid to a specific partner → allocated to that partner

**Non-business income** (e.g., rental received by partnership):
- NOT included in PAI from business
- Separate source taxed under Section 4(d)
- Adjusted income from non-business sources apportioned among partners using same profit sharing ratio

---

## 4. Profit Sharing Ratio Changes Mid-Year

If profit sharing arrangement changes during the basis period:
- Divisible income is deemed to have accrued evenly over the basis period
- Allocated on a time basis to periods before and after the change
- Each period's divisible income divided per the arrangements during that period
- Amounts aggregated for each partner

---

## 5. Capital Allowances

**Paragraph 74, Schedule 3**

- Computed for the partnership by reference to capital expenditure incurred on assets in the basis period
- Asset must be owned by the partnership AND in use for business at end of basis period
- Allocated among partners per profit sharing ratio **at the end of the basis period**
- Unabsorbed CA carried forward to following YA

### Partner's Own Capital Expenditure (Section 13)
If a partner personally finances an asset (e.g., motor vehicle) used wholly in partnership business → CA given wholly to that partner.

### Changes in Partners
- CA allocated only among partners who are still partners at year-end
- New partner admitted and remaining at year-end gets full CA
- Partner who leaves before year-end gets NO CA for that year

**At partner level (Section 44):**
- Current year business loss: set off against ALL sources [s.44(2)]
- Unabsorbed: carried forward, deductible against statutory income from business sources only
- Maximum carry-forward: 10 consecutive years (from YA 2019)

## 7. Filing Obligations

### Who Files
- The **precedent partner** [Section 86(1)(a)]
- Or any attorney/agent/manager/factor in Malaysia if no acting partner present

### Precedent Partner (Section 86(1)(a))
The partner who, being an acting partner present in Malaysia:
- Is the first named in a partnership agreement; or
- If no agreement, is specified by name/initial singly or with precedence

### Form CP30
Precedent partner must issue the **Apportionment of Partnership Income in Form CP30** to each partner, enabling them to declare partnership income.

### Deadline
- **30 June** of the following year
- E-filing available

### Dissolved Partnership (Section 86(3))
Persons who were partners immediately before dissolution are deemed to continue as partners for Form P filing purposes.

## 8. Salary/Interest to Partners - Section 36(4)

Payments to partners are NOT deductible at entity level. The add-back/deduct/allocate mechanism:

1. **Added back** in computing PAI
2. **Subtracted** to arrive at divisible income
3. **Allocated directly** to the specific partner

Net effect: partner taxed on these amounts as part of partnership income, NOT as employment income.

### Interest on Private Loans by Partners
Same mechanism — added back, deducted, allocated to specific partner.

### Salaried Partners - Section 4(b) vs 4(a)
A person receiving fixed salary without sharing losses and having no rights in goodwill or direction may actually be an employee. If so, income taxed under Section 4(b) (employment), not 4(a) (business). This is a question of fact.

---

## 9. Corporate Partners vs Individual Partners

### Individual Partners
- Income taxed at individual progressive rates
- Entitled to personal reliefs
- File Form B (deadline: 30 June)
- Business losses can offset other personal income sources

### Corporate Partners
- Partnership income taxed as Section 4(a) business income
- Taxed at corporate rates (24% standard; SME graduated rates if qualifying)
- File Form C (deadline: 7 months from FYE)
- Cannot claim personal reliefs

---

## 10. Conversion from Partnership to Sdn Bhd

### Capital Allowances
Where partnership business taken over by company and treated as one continuing business (Section 55/subsection 19(6)):
- If at least one partner continues as shareholder/director carrying on substantially the same business
- CA and charges calculated as if conducted by same person throughout
- Assets deemed transferred at tax written-down value (residual expenditure)

### Stock/Inventory
Transferred at market value. Difference between book value and market value is a taxable adjustment.

### Business Losses
- Unabsorbed partnership losses CANNOT be transferred to the new Sdn Bhd
- They die with the partnership/individual partners

### Balancing Adjustments
Disposal of assets on cessation triggers balancing allowance or balancing charge.

### Pre-Commencement Expenses
Expenses on memorandum & articles of association and company registration are deductible under relevant Rules.
