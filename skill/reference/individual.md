# Individual Taxation — Comprehensive

Covers residence determination, employment income (s.13), benefits-in-kind, PCB reconciliation, expatriate schemes, all income sources (s.4(a)-(f)), personal reliefs, rebates, and the 2% dividend tax.

---

## Residence Determination [s.7 ITA]

Residence status changes EVERYTHING — progressive rates vs 30% flat, personal reliefs available vs none, exemptions available vs none.

### The Tests (applied in order)

```
TEST 1 — 182 days [s.7(1)(a)]:
  Individual is in Malaysia for 182 days or more in a basis year.
  → RESIDENT for that YA.
  
TEST 2 — Short period linked to 182 [s.7(1)(b)]:
  Individual is in Malaysia for a period of less than 182 days in a basis year, AND
  that period is linked to a period of 182+ consecutive days in an adjacent year
  by the individual's physical presence in Malaysia.
  → RESIDENT for that YA.
  "Linked" means: no break of more than 14 consecutive days.

TEST 3 — 90 + 90 rule [s.7(1)(c)]:
  Individual is in Malaysia for 90 days or more in a basis year, AND
  was in Malaysia for 90+ days in any 3 of the preceding 4 basis years.
  → RESIDENT for that YA.

TEST 4 — Resident in following year and 3 preceding [s.7(1)(d)]:
  Individual is resident for the basis year FOLLOWING that year, AND
  was resident for 3 basis years immediately preceding that year.
  → RESIDENT for that year (even if physical presence is minimal).
```

### Temporary absence [s.7(1) proviso]

```
The following do NOT break physical presence:
  (a) Connected with the individual's service in Malaysia (business trips)
  (b) Due to ill-health of the individual or immediate family
  (c) In respect of social visits not exceeding 14 days in aggregate

NOTE: Must be TEMPORARY absence from Malaysia. Extended overseas postings
do not qualify even if related to Malaysian employment.
```

### Day counting rules

```
- Day of arrival IN Malaysia = counted
- Day of departure FROM Malaysia = counted
- Transit (not passing immigration) = NOT counted
- Part of a day = counted as a full day
```

## Employment Income [s.13(1)]

### Gross employment income components

```
s.13(1)(a): Wages, salary, remuneration, leave pay, fees, commissions,
            bonuses, gratuities, perquisites (cash benefits)

s.13(1)(b): Value of BIK (benefit-in-kind) provided by employer
            → Prescribed value (fixed amount) OR formula (annual value)

s.13(1)(c): Value of living accommodation provided by employer
            → 30% of s.13(1)(a) cash remuneration (capped at actual rent/value)
            → Or actual value if provided rent-free by employer

s.13(1)(d): Amount from unapproved pension/provident fund employer contribution

s.13(1)(e): Compensation for loss of employment (retirement/retrenchment)
            → Exempt up to RM20,000 per complete year of service [Para 15, Schedule 6]
            → Only if due to ill-health, redundancy, or attaining 55/compulsory retirement age
            → Excess above exemption = taxable
```

### Formula method (alternative to prescribed)

```
ANNUAL VALUE = Cost to employer / estimated useful life
OR
ANNUAL VALUE = Market rental value (if rented by employer for employee)

The LOWER of prescribed value or formula value may be used — whichever benefits the taxpayer.
```

### BIK exemptions (not taxable)

```
□ Dental benefit
□ Childcare benefit (employer-provided facility or RM2,400/year childcare allowance)
□ Food/drink provided free
□ Free transport between home and work (group transport)
□ Obligatory insurance premiums for foreign workers
□ Mobile phone, notebook, tablet (one unit each, used for work)
□ Parking
□ Employer's goods/services provided free/at discount (≤ RM1,000/year)
□ Medical benefits (including maternity, traditional medicine)
□ Leave passage:
    - Domestic: up to 3× per year
    - Overseas: 1× per year (max RM3,000 inclusive of meals/accommodation)
□ Monthly bills for broadband, telephone (registered under employer's name)
□ Petrol card/petrol allowance/toll (up to RM6,000/year — BUT only if NO motor car BIK claimed)
```

### Company car BIK — the most common issue

```
STANDARD RULE:
  BIK = prescribed value based on cost of car when new
  + fuel card/allowance (RM6,000 or actual if lower)
  - Car provided > 5 years: prescribed value × 50%

EMPLOYEE CONTRIBUTES TO COST:
  If employee pays part of running cost → deduct from BIK value

CAR OWNED BY EMPLOYEE, EMPLOYER REIMBURSES:
  → NOT a BIK. Taxed as cash perquisite under s.13(1)(a).
  → Mileage claims up to LHDN rates exempt; excess is taxable.

POOLED CARS (not assigned to specific employee):
  → Not a BIK if genuinely pooled for business use only
```

## Living Accommodation [s.13(1)(c)]

```
VALUE = LOWER of:
  (a) 30% of gross employment income (s.13(1)(a) amount)
  (b) Defined value of premises

DEFINED VALUE:
  - If employer-owned: market rental value
  - If employer-rented: actual rent paid by employer

SHARED ACCOMMODATION: apportion between employees sharing

EXEMPTIONS:
  - Hotel accommodation (temporary, ≤ 3 months during relocation)
  - Accommodation at source of employment if location > 50km from nearest town
    with equivalent accommodation (remote area exemption — e.g., plantation estates)

FURNITURE ADDITION:
  If accommodation provided furnished:
  → Add prescribed furniture BIK (or actual annual value, whichever lower)
```

## PCB/MTD Reconciliation

```
PCB (Potongan Cukai Bulanan) / MTD (Monthly Tax Deduction):
  Employer deducts estimated tax monthly and remits to LHDN by 15th of following month.

AT YEAR END:
  Actual tax liability (from Form BE/B/M computation)
  Less: Total PCB deducted during the year
  = Balance payable / (refundable)

COMMON ISSUES:
  □ PCB over-deducted: refund via Form BE filing
  □ PCB under-deducted: balance payable by 30 April (no penalty if ≤ RM2,000 shortfall and PCB made)
  □ Employer penalty for wrong PCB computation: s.107(3) — employer liable for shortfall
  □ Employee changed jobs mid-year: new employer may not have prior PCB data
  □ Multiple employment: each employer deducts independently — aggregate may be under-deducted

FORM CP22A (cessation of employment):
  Employer must notify LHDN 30 days BEFORE non-citizen employee leaves Malaysia.
  SPC (tax clearance) must be obtained before final payment to employee.
```

## Expatriate Schemes

### Returning Expert Programme (REP)

```
ELIGIBILITY: Malaysian citizen residing overseas who returns to Malaysia
BENEFIT: Flat 15% on employment income for 5 consecutive YAs
CONDITIONS:
  - Must be 45 years old or below at application date (Budget 2026: raised to 50)
  - Employed in a C-suite or key position
  - Minimum 3 years continuous residence overseas
  - Return between specified dates (application to TalentCorp)
```

### Knowledge Worker (Iskandar Malaysia)

```
ELIGIBILITY: Knowledge worker in specified sectors in Iskandar Malaysia
BENEFIT: Flat 15% on employment income for 5 consecutive YAs
SECTORS: Creative/technology/knowledge-based industries designated by IRDA
STATUS: Being phased into JS-SEZ framework (check current gazette)
```

### Short-term employment

```
IF non-resident individual works in Malaysia for ≤ 60 days in a calendar year:
  → Employment income is EXEMPT [Para 21, Schedule 6]
  → Does NOT count toward 182-day residence test

IF 61-181 days (non-resident):
  → Taxed at FLAT 30% (no personal reliefs, no progressive rates)
  → No double deductions, no exemptions for BIK

IF ≥ 182 days:
  → Resident. Progressive rates. Full reliefs available.
```

## All Income Sources

### Section 4 income categories for individuals

| Section | Source | Treatment |
|---------|--------|-----------|
| s.4(a) | Business | Adjusted income after deductions. Progressive rates. Reliefs available. |
| s.4(b) | Employment | Gross employment income (s.13) less permitted deductions (limited). |
| s.4(c) | Dividends | Single-tier: exempt. Others: gross up + tax credit. 2% tax on >RM100k (YA 2025+). |
| s.4(d) | Interest | Generally exempt (bank deposits, approved bonds). Taxable if from related party or non-exempt source. |
| s.4(e) | Discounts | Rarely relevant. Original issue discount on bonds. |
| s.4(f) | Rents, royalties, premiums | Rental: deductions available (assessment, quit rent, repairs, fire insurance, interest). Royalty: normally WHT already deducted for non-residents. |

## Personal Reliefs [YA 2025-2026]

Load from `data/reliefs.json`. Key points for practitioner:

```
CRITICAL RULES:
  □ Reliefs available ONLY to tax residents
  □ Non-residents: ZERO reliefs (flat 30% on gross income)
  □ Reliefs deducted from TOTAL INCOME to arrive at CHARGEABLE INCOME
  □ Cannot create negative chargeable income (excess relief = wasted)
  □ Spouse relief: only ONE spouse claims (either RM4,000 or joint assessment)
  □ Child relief: only ONE parent claims per child
  □ Medical: RM10,000 aggregate (parents + self/spouse/child medical combined)
  □ Education (self): RM7,000 (Masters/PhD/professional/upskilling — specific list)
  □ Lifestyle: RM2,500 (books, internet, sports equipment, gym, electronics — combined)
```

## Practitioner Traps

```
 1. BIK NOT DECLARED: LHDN crosses-references Form EA with employee's Form BE. Missing BIK = audit.
 2. RESIDENCE ASSUMED: Don't assume client is resident. Count the days. 181 days = non-resident.
 3. SPOUSE BOTH CLAIMING: Only one spouse can claim child relief. Check joint vs separate assessment.
 4. MEDICAL RECEIPTS: RM10,000 cap is AGGREGATE. Parents' medical + self medical share the cap.
 5. COMPANY CAR + PETROL: If motor car BIK is declared, petrol exemption is included (RM6,000).
    Cannot claim petrol exemption AND no car BIK.
 6. COMPENSATION ON TERMINATION: Only exempt (RM20k × years) if genuinely for health/redundancy/retirement.
    "Garden leave" payment = taxable employment income in full.
 7. FOREIGN INCOME: Individual resident — foreign employment income exercised in Malaysia = taxable.
    Foreign investment income received in Malaysia = exempt (from YA 2022, extended to 2030).
 8. EP REFUND: Employees above 60 receiving EPF withdrawal — this is NOT taxable income.
 9. DUAL EMPLOYMENT: Each employer issues Form EA separately. Employee aggregates ALL.
10. DEEMED NON-RESIDENT: Employee posted overseas full year but s.7(1)(d) makes them resident —
    employment income for services rendered OUTSIDE Malaysia is still exempt [Para 28, Schedule 6].
```

## NEVER

- File an individual computation without first confirming residence status (day count)
- Assume company car BIK at prescribed value without checking if formula method is lower
- Forget the 2% dividend tax for individuals with >RM100k dividend income (from YA 2025)
- Apply personal reliefs to a non-resident individual
- Double-count reliefs between spouses without determining who claims what
- Ignore PCB already deducted when computing balance payable
- Treat all compensation on termination as exempt (only specific qualifying scenarios)
