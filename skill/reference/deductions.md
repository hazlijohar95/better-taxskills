# Allowable & Disallowable Expenses - Complete Reference

**Legislative basis:** ITA 1967, Sections 33, 34, 39
**Key Public Rulings:** PR 3/2008 (Entertainment), PR 6/2006 (Legal Fees), PR 1/2002 (Bad Debts), PR 12/2019 (Forex), PR 1/2013 (Export Promotion), PR 5/2004 (R&D), PR 1/2005 (Self-Assessment)
**Key Subsidiary Legislation:** P.U.(A) 336/2014 (Secretarial/Tax Filing Fees)
**Last updated:** May 2026 (Budget 2026 double deduction extensions incorporated in 07-incentives.md)

---

## 1. The General Deduction Test (Section 33)

### Statutory Text
> "...all outgoings and expenses wholly and exclusively incurred during that period by that person in the production of gross income from that source."

### Four-Part Test (ALL must be satisfied)

1. **Outgoings and expenses** — actual expenditure (not provisions, reserves, notional charges)
2. **Wholly and exclusively** — entire amount for business; dual-purpose fails unless apportionable
3. **Incurred during that period** — relates to the basis period (accrual basis)
4. **In the production of gross income from that source** — direct nexus to income-producing activity

### Revenue vs Capital

| Characteristic | Revenue (Deductible) | Capital (Not Deductible) |
|----------------|---------------------|--------------------------|
| Nature | Recurring, operational | One-off, enduring benefit |
| Purpose | Maintaining profit-earning capacity | Creating/acquiring profit-earning capacity |
| Asset created | No enduring asset | Creates or enhances enduring asset |
| Example | Repairs to factory roof | Building new factory extension |
| Tax treatment | Section 33(1) deduction | Capital allowance (Schedule 3) if qualifying |

## 2. Specifically Enumerated Deductions (Section 33(1))

### Section 33(1)(a) — Interest Expense
Interest on money borrowed and:
- (i) employed in production of gross income from that source; OR
- (ii) laid out on assets used/held for production of gross income

Subject to subsection 33(2) restriction where money partly used for non-business.

### Section 33(1)(b) — Rent
Rent payable for land/building occupied for producing gross income from that source.

### Section 33(1)(c) — Repairs
Expenses for repair of premises, plant, machinery, fixtures; renewal/repair/alteration of implements, utensils, articles.

**Excluding:**
- Items where expenditure would be qualifying plant expenditure (Schedule 3)
- Any means of conveyance
- Reconstructing/rebuilding premises, buildings, permanent structures, plant/machinery, fixtures

### Section 33(1)(d) — Prescribed Deductions
Gateway for subsidiary legislation granting specific deductions.

## 4. Specifically Disallowed (Section 39)

| Section | Item | Details |
|---------|------|---------|
| 39(1)(a) | Domestic/private expenses | Personal/household of taxpayer |
| 39(1)(b) | Capital expenditure | Unless specifically allowed elsewhere |
| 39(1)(c) | Depreciation | Replaced by capital allowances |
| 39(1)(d) | Not wholly and exclusively for business | Fails the nexus test |
| 39(1)(e) | Income tax or similar taxes | Malaysian or foreign income tax |
| 39(1)(f) | Unapproved scheme contributions | Pension/provident/savings not approved by DGIR |
| 39(1)(g) | Employer's own pension/retirement | |
| 39(1)(h) | Leave passage exceeding limits | |
| 39(1)(l) | Entertainment (50% restriction) | General rule |
| 39(1)(m) | General provisions | Only specific bad debts allowed |

### Section 39(2)/(3) — WHT Consequences
If payment to non-resident is subject to withholding tax and the payer fails to deduct/remit WHT → the expense is NOT deductible until WHT is paid.

### Common Disallowances in Practice
- Penalties, fines, summonses, compound payments for breach of law
- Donations to non-approved bodies
- Political contributions/donations
- Personal motor vehicle expenses (non-business portion)
- General provisions (doubtful debts percentage)
- Depreciation/amortisation (per accounts — must add back)
- Interest on borrowings not used for income production
- Legal fees for capital matters (acquisition, corporate restructuring)
- Goodwill amortisation
- Formation expenses (but see pre-commencement rules)
- Share issue costs
- Dividends paid

## 5. Entertainment Expenses (PR 3/2008)

### Three-Step Decision Process

**Step 1:** Is it entertainment? → If no, deduct in full under Section 33.
**Step 2:** Is it wholly and exclusively for business? → If no, 100% disallowed under Section 39(1)(d).
**Step 3:** Does it fall within the provisos? → If yes, 100% deductible. If no, 50% deductible.

### What Counts as Entertainment (Section 18 definition)
- Provision of food, drink, recreation, hospitality
- In connection with business — to clients, customers, suppliers, business contacts

### 100% Deductible (Within Provisos)

| Scenario | Treatment |
|----------|-----------|
| Entertainment for employees | 100% allowed |
| Promotional samples/gifts bearing company name/logo | 100% allowed |
| Entertainment at trade fairs/exhibitions | 100% allowed |
| Entertainment of customers incidental to employee entertainment | 100% allowed |
| Food/beverage/entertainment company (as part of business operations) | 100% allowed |
| Corporate hospitality sponsorship (with advertising benefit) | 100% allowed |
| Entertainment in home/personal restaurant providing to customers | 100% allowed |
| Samples for certification/quality testing | 100% allowed |

### 50% Deductible (Not Within Provisos)
- Client/customer entertainment (dinners, golf, etc.)
- Entertainment of suppliers/business associates
- Entertainment at company premises for non-employees
- Any entertainment not falling within the provisos above

### NOT Entertainment (Full Deduction as Normal Expense)
- Telephone/communication costs
- Travel and accommodation (for business meetings)
- Office refreshments for staff (tea, coffee)
- Working lunches (internal, no external parties)

### Key Traps
- Members' spouse/parents/children treated as NON-members in clubs
- Mixed events (staff + clients): apportion between 100% and 50% portions
- Promotional gifts > RM500/unit: may lose proviso protection [VERIFY current threshold]

## 7. Bad Debts and Doubtful Debts (PR 1/2002)

### Deductible — Specific Bad Debts

Conditions for deduction:
1. Debt must have been included in gross income (traded as revenue)
2. Debt must be specifically identified
3. Efforts to recover must have been made and are reasonably believed irrecoverable
4. Debt must be written off in the accounts

### Not Deductible — General Provisions
- Percentage-based provisions (e.g., 2% of debtors)
- Provisions based on ageing analysis without specific identification
- "Provision for doubtful debts" in accounts → add back

### Recovery of Bad Debts
Previously allowed bad debts that are subsequently recovered → taxable income in year of recovery.

## 8. Interest Expense and Restriction

### Section 33(1)(a) — Basic Deductibility
Interest on money borrowed and employed in production of gross income from that source.

### Section 33(2) — Partial Use Restriction
Where borrowed money used partly for non-business purposes:
Formula restricts deductible interest proportionately.

### Section 33(4) — Timing
Interest accrued but not yet paid: deductible when it accrues (accrual basis).

### Section 140C — Earnings Stripping Rules
- Applies to interest > RM500,000 in controlled cross-border transactions
- Maximum deduction: 20% of tax-EBITDA per business source
- Excess carried forward indefinitely (subject to shareholders' continuity)

## 9. Foreign Exchange Gains and Losses (PR 12/2019)

### Decision Matrix

| Type | Revenue/Capital | Realised/Unrealised | Treatment |
|------|----------------|--------------------:|-----------|
| Trade (debtors/creditors) | Revenue | Realised | Taxable gain / Deductible loss |
| Trade (debtors/creditors) | Revenue | Unrealised | Taxable gain / Deductible loss |
| Non-trade (loans/investments) | Capital | Realised | Not taxable / Not deductible |
| Non-trade (loans/investments) | Capital | Unrealised | Not taxable / Not deductible |

**Key principle:** Trade-related forex (from normal business transactions with debtors/creditors) is revenue → both gains and losses are revenue items. Non-trade (capital borrowings, investment holdings) is capital → no tax effect.

**Unrealised forex on trade items:** From YA 2019, unrealised gains on trade items are taxable and unrealised losses are deductible (change from prior position).

## 11. Repairs vs Capital Improvement

### The Test
- **Repair:** Restoring asset to original condition → deductible
- **Improvement:** Enhancing, upgrading, adding new capability → capital (not deductible, may be CA)

### Practical Examples

| Expense | Treatment | Reason |
|---------|-----------|--------|
| Replacing broken roof tiles (same type) | Repair (deductible) | Restoration |
| Upgrading roof to better material | Capital (not deductible) | Enhancement |
| Repainting factory walls | Repair | Maintenance |
| Adding new floor to building | Capital | New asset/extension |
| Replacing worn engine (same spec) | Repair | Restoration |
| Engine upgrade to higher spec | Capital | Enhancement |
| Patching potholes in parking lot | Repair | Maintenance |
| Resurfacing entire car park with new material | Capital | Improvement |

## 13. Double Deductions (Section 34)

### Export Promotion (PR 1/2013, Section 34(6))
200% of:
- Overseas advertising expenditure
- Supply of free samples abroad
- Trade fair/exhibition participation abroad
- Maintaining overseas trade offices
- Market research for export

### Research & Development (Section 34A, PR 5/2004)
200% of:
- Payments to approved research institutes/companies
- In-house R&D expenditure (must be approved by MOSTI)
- Cash contributions to approved research institutes

### Training of Employees
200% of:
- Training costs at approved institutions
- HRDF levy utilisation
- Specific approved training programmes

### Employment of Disabled Persons (Section 34(6)(e))
200% of remuneration paid to disabled employees registered with Department of Social Welfare (JKM).

### Environmental Protection
200% of qualifying environmental protection expenditure per gazette orders.

### Halal Certification
200% of expenditure for JAKIM/local religious authority halal certification.

### Internship Programme
200% of expenditure on structured internship with institutions of higher learning (conditions apply).

## 14. Hire Purchase Transactions

### Interest
- HP interest (finance charge) is revenue expenditure → deductible when incurred
- Allocated over the HP term using sum-of-digits or straight-line method per accounts

### Capital Portion
- Not deductible as expense (capital expenditure)
- Qualifies for capital allowance under Schedule 3

### Depreciation
- Accounting depreciation on HP asset: add back (Section 39(1)(c))
- Claim capital allowance instead

---

## 15. Withholding Tax and Deductibility (Section 39(2)/(3))

### Rule
If payer fails to deduct/remit withholding tax on payments to non-residents → the expense is NOT deductible until WHT is paid and remitted to LHDN.

### Practical Impact
- Technical fees to non-residents: must deduct 10% WHT
- Royalties to non-residents: must deduct 10% WHT
- Interest to non-residents: must deduct 15% WHT
- Contract payments: must deduct 13% (10% + 3%)

If WHT not remitted within 1 month → 10% penalty AND expense disallowed until paid.

## 16. Donations, Zakat, and Gifts

### Section 44(6) — Approved Donations
- Cash donations to Section 44(6) approved institutions
- Deduction: capped at **10% of aggregate income**
- Must have valid tax-deductible receipt

### Government Donations
- Donations to federal/state/local government
- Fully deductible, no percentage cap
- Requires Kew-38 receipt

### Company Zakat — Section 44(11A)
- Zakat paid by companies to approved religious bodies
- Deductible up to **2.5% of aggregate income**

### Individual Zakat — Section 6A(3)
- Tax rebate (not deduction)
- Dollar-for-dollar reduction of tax charged
- Limited to total tax charged

### Non-Approved Donations
- NOT deductible
- Add back in full

## 18. Practical Classification Flowchart

```
EXPENSE INCURRED
    │
    ├── Is it domestic/private? ──────────── YES → 100% Disallowed [s.39(1)(a)]
    │
    ├── Is it capital in nature? ──────────── YES → Not deductible [s.39(1)(b)]
    │   │                                            └── May qualify for CA (Schedule 3)
    │   │
    ├── Is it depreciation? ──────────────── YES → Add back [s.39(1)(c)]
    │   │                                            └── Claim CA instead
    │   │
    ├── Is it entertainment? ──────────────── YES → Apply PR 3/2008 3-step test
    │   │                                            ├── Within provisos → 100%
    │   │                                            └── Not within → 50%
    │   │
    ├── Is it a general provision? ─────────── YES → Add back [s.39(1)(m)]
    │   │
    ├── Is it income tax? ─────────────────── YES → Add back [s.39(1)(e)]
    │   │
    ├── WHT obligation met? ───────────────── NO → Disallowed until paid [s.39(2)]
    │   │
    ├── Double deduction eligible? ─────────── YES → 200% deduction [s.34]
    │   │
    └── Wholly and exclusively for business? ── YES → Deductible [s.33(1)]
                                                 NO → Disallowed [s.39(1)(d)]
```
