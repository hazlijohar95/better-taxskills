# Group Taxation & Consolidation — Malaysia

**Authority:** Income Tax Act 1967 (Sections 44A, 60F, 140A); Schedule 3 Paragraphs 38-40, 75A-75C; Stamp Act 1949 (Section 15A); LHDN Public Rulings  
**Effective:** YA 2025/2026 (with Budget 2026 changes)

---

## 1. FUNDAMENTAL PRINCIPLE: NO TAX CONSOLIDATION IN MALAYSIA

**Malaysia does NOT allow consolidated tax filing.** Each company in a group is a SEPARATE taxpayer — files its own Form C, computes its own tax independently.

There is NO:
- Consolidated return (like US 1120)
- Tax unity/fiscal unity (like Netherlands/Germany)
- Group consolidation regime (like Australia)
- Pooled losses or group-wide offset

**Each company must:**
- File its own Form C by the 7th month after FYE
- Prepare its own tax computation
- Pay its own CP204 instalments
- Maintain its own loss memorandum
- Claim its own capital allowances

**The ONLY relief for groups is Section 44A (group relief) — and it is HIGHLY RESTRICTIVE.**

## 2. GROUP RELIEF — SECTION 44A

### 2.1 What It Allows

A company (surrendering company) may surrender up to **70% of its adjusted loss** to a related company (claimant company) in the SAME year of assessment.

This is NOT a general loss transfer — it operates only under strict conditions with severe limitations.

### 2.2 ALL Conditions (Every Single One Must Be Met)

| # | Condition | Detail |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Both companies incorporated in Malaysia | Under Companies Act 2016 |
| 2 | Both companies tax-resident in Malaysia | Management and control in MY |
| 3 | Both have paid-up capital > RM2.5 million | At beginning of basis period |
| 4 | Both have same accounting period | Exact same 12-month period |
| 5 | 70% direct/indirect ownership | Common parent or one owns the other |
| 6 | Surrendering company in first 3 YAs | From date of commencement of business |
| 7 | Both are NOT enjoying tax incentives | No PS, ITA, RA, or any incentive |
| 8 | Claimant company has chargeable income | Must have income to absorb the loss |
| 9 | Loss is adjusted loss only | NOT unabsorbed CA, NOT capital losses |
| 10 | Maximum surrender: 70% of adjusted loss | Cannot transfer 100% of loss |

### 2.3 Ownership Test — 70% Threshold

The 70% relationship can be:
- **Direct:** Company A owns ≥70% of Company B
- **Indirect via common parent:** Parent owns ≥70% of both Company A and Company B
- **Multi-tier:** Parent → 70% → Intermediate → 70% → Sub = qualifies (effective: 70% × 70% = 49% — BUT LHDN applies 70% at EACH tier, so this DOES qualify)

**Ownership means:** ordinary share capital carrying ≥70% of voting power and ≥70% of distributable profits and ≥70% of assets on winding-up.

### 2.4 "First 3 YAs" Limitation

The surrendering company can ONLY surrender losses incurred in its **first 3 years of assessment** from commencement of business:
- YA of commencement = Year 1
- Next consecutive YA = Year 2  
- Following YA = Year 3
- From Year 4 onwards: NO group relief available (losses trapped in the company)

### 2.5 Practical Impact

Group relief is almost NEVER applicable for established groups because:
- Most companies are well past their first 3 YAs
- Both companies must have paid-up capital >RM2.5m (excludes many SMEs)
- Both must have exact same FYE (not just same length)
- Neither can have any incentive
- Only 70% of loss transferable (30% remains trapped)

**Real-world use case:** Large group sets up a new subsidiary that will generate start-up losses. These losses may be group-relieved to a profitable sister company for the first 3 years only.

### 2.6 Application Procedure

- Surrendering company: include in Form C (specify amount surrendered and claimant company)
- Claimant company: include in Form C (specify amount claimed and surrendering company)
- No separate application to LHDN required
- Both Forms must be filed on time
- Joint election: both companies must agree (implied by both declaring in Form C)

## 3. CONTROLLED TRANSFERS — SCHEDULE 3 PARAGRAPHS 38-40

### 3.1 Purpose

Allows **tax-neutral** transfers of assets between related companies within a group — specifically, NO balancing charge on the transferor and NO IA for the transferee.

### 3.2 Conditions for Para 38 (Controlled Sale)

| Condition | Requirement |
|---|---|
| Relationship | Transferor and transferee are "controlled persons" |
| Asset type | Asset on which CA has been claimed |
| Election | Both parties must jointly elect Para 38 treatment |
| Purpose | Not primarily for tax avoidance |

**"Controlled persons"** (Para 39): Two companies where:
- One controls the other (≥50% share capital), OR
- Both are controlled by the same person/company

### 3.3 Tax Treatment Under Para 38

```
TRANSFEROR:
- Deemed disposal price = TAX WRITTEN DOWN VALUE (TWDV) at date of transfer
- Therefore: NO balancing charge and NO balancing allowance
- Asset removed from transferor's CA schedule at TWDV

TRANSFEREE:
- Deemed cost = TWDV of transferor
- NO initial allowance (IA) on the transferred asset
- Annual allowance continues at same rate, on carried-over TWDV
- Residual expenditure = transferor's TWDV at transfer date
```

### 3.4 Clawback Under Para 40

If within **2 years** of the controlled transfer:
- The transferee disposes of the asset to a non-connected person, OR
- The control relationship ceases (ownership falls below 50%)

Then:
- The transfer is UNWOUND for tax purposes
- Transferor assessed as if sold at market value (balancing charge arises)
- Transferee's CA is recalculated from scratch
- Additional assessments may be raised on both parties

### 3.5 Practical Application

| Scenario | Para 38 Available? |
|---|---|
| Parent transfers factory to subsidiary (80% owned) | YES |
| Subsidiary transfers equipment to sister company (both 100% owned by parent) | YES |
| Company transfers asset to joint venture (50% owned) | YES (50% meets "controls") |
| Transfer to company 40% owned | NO — below 50% |
| Transfer of land (no CA claimed) | NO — Para 38 only applies to CA assets |
| Transfer of stock-in-trade | NO — not a CA asset |

## 4. INVESTMENT HOLDING COMPANY (IHC) — SECTION 60F

### 4.1 Definition

A company whose activities consist **wholly** of the holding of investments AND whose income is derived from investment sources (dividends, interest, rental):
- Not carrying on any trade or business
- Income: dividends, interest, rent, gains on disposal of investments

### 4.2 Special Tax Treatment

IHC is taxed under **Section 60F** — a restrictive regime:

```
RULES:
1. Each investment source assessed SEPARATELY
2. Expenses deductible ONLY against the specific source they relate to
3. Common/administrative expenses: APPORTIONED across sources
   (Usually by proportion of gross income from each source)
4. Unabsorbed expenses CANNOT be carried forward
   → Use it or lose it, per year, per source
5. Losses in one source CANNOT offset income from another source
6. Capital allowances: available only against rental income (if applicable)
7. No group relief (IHC has no "adjusted loss" from business)
```

### 4.3 IHC vs Operating Company

| Feature | IHC (s.60F) | Operating Company |
|---|---|---|
| Business source income | NO | YES |
| Loss carry-forward | NO | YES (10 years) |
| Inter-source offset | NO | YES |
| Group relief | NO | YES (if conditions met) |
| Tax rate | 24% | 24% (or SME tiered) |
| SME rate | NOT AVAILABLE | Available if qualifies |
| CP204 | Required | Required |
| Capital allowances | Limited (rental assets only) | Full |
| Double deductions | NOT AVAILABLE | Available |

### 4.4 Avoiding IHC Trap

A company that holds investments BUT ALSO carries on a trade/business is NOT an IHC:
- Even minimal trading activity can prevent s.60F classification
- Property company renting own properties: IHC (passive rental)
- Property company developing + selling properties: NOT IHC (trade)
- Mixed: depends on predominant activity (LHDN scrutinises)

### 4.5 Intermediate Holding Companies in Groups

Groups commonly have intermediate holding companies (HoldCos) that hold shares in operating subsidiaries:
- If HoldCo's ONLY income is dividends from subsidiaries → IHC
- Single-tier dividends = exempt → no income to assess → s.60F restricts expense claims
- Management fees charged DOWN to subsidiaries → business activity → potentially NOT IHC
- Careful: LHDN may argue fees are contrived to avoid IHC classification

## 5. INTRA-GROUP TRANSACTIONS

### 5.1 Transfer Pricing Applies

ALL transactions between related companies in a group are subject to **Section 140A** (arm's length principle):
- Management fees charged between group companies
- Interest on inter-company loans
- Rental of premises/equipment between related parties
- Sale of goods (cost-plus, resale price adjustments)
- Service charges and cost allocations

### 5.2 Common Intra-Group Transactions and LHDN Focus

| Transaction | LHDN Challenge Area |
|---|---|
| Management fees (parent → sub) | Must demonstrate real services rendered, at arm's length rate |
| Shared service centre charges | Must have proper cost allocation methodology (documented) |
| Interest on inter-company loan | Rate must be arm's length; earnings stripping [s.140C] applies |
| Royalties (brand/IP licence) | Must show IP has value; compare to third-party licence rates |
| Cost-plus services | Mark-up must be comparable to independent providers |
| Guarantee fees | Must be charged; comparable to bank guarantee pricing |

### 5.3 Management Fee Deductibility

For management fees paid to related parties to be deductible:
1. **Real services** must be provided (not just paper invoices)
2. **Benefit test**: receiving company must derive actual benefit
3. **Documentation**: service agreement, description of services, time records
4. **Arm's length rate**: comparable to independent third-party providers
5. **Not duplicated**: receiving company should not already have staff doing same work
6. **Shareholder costs excluded**: parent's costs of being a shareholder (board oversight, listing requirements) are NOT chargeable to subsidiaries

### 5.4 Inter-Company Loans

```
RULES:
1. Interest rate must be arm's length (compare: bank borrowing rates for similar risk)
2. Earnings stripping [s.140C]:
   - Interest paid to related party: deduction capped at 20% of tax-EBITDA
   - De minimis: RM500,000 (total related-party interest)
   - Excess: permanently disallowed (NO carry-forward)
3. Thin capitalisation: abolished from YA 2018 (replaced by earnings stripping)
4. Interest-free loans: LHDN may impute interest at arm's length
5. WHT: if lender is non-resident → 15% WHT on interest [s.109] (or DTA rate)
```

### 5.5 Dividends Within Group

- Single-tier dividends: EXEMPT in the hands of recipient [s.108 single-tier system]
- No WHT on dividends paid to Malaysian resident companies
- IHC receiving dividends: exempt income (no tax, but also no expense deduction against it)
- Parent receiving dividends from foreign subsidiary: FSI rules apply (taxable on remittance unless exempt)

## 6. SHAREHOLDER CONTINUITY — PARAGRAPHS 75A-75C (SCHEDULE 3)

### 6.1 The Rule

If there is a **substantial change in shareholders** of a company:
- Unabsorbed capital allowances → MAY BE FORFEITED
- Unabsorbed business losses → MAY BE FORFEITED

### 6.2 What Constitutes "Substantial Change"

A change in shareholders such that:
- More than 50% of the ordinary share capital is held by different persons from those who held it in the year the losses/CA originated

**Measured:** Comparing shareholders at point of claiming B/F losses or CA vs shareholders in the year the losses/CA arose.

### 6.3 Exception — Same Business Test

Losses and CA are PRESERVED despite shareholder change IF:
- The company continues to carry on the **same business** as when the losses were incurred
- No dormancy between the change and the claim year
- Business remains substantially the same in nature and scale

### 6.4 Practical Issues in Group Restructuring

| Event | Impact on Losses/CA |
|---|---|
| Parent sells subsidiary to external buyer | New shareholders → test triggered → may forfeit |
| Internal restructuring (share swap) within same group | If ultimate beneficial owners unchanged → usually safe |
| Company dormant > 1 year then revived by new owner | All B/F losses and CA LOST (Para 75C) |
| New shares issued (dilution > 50%) | Triggers the test |
| Conversion to PLT | Para 75A designed for companies — PLT has own rules |

## 7. LOSS RING-FENCING IN GROUPS

### 7.1 No Group Loss Pooling

Each company's losses are ring-fenced within that company:
- Cannot transfer losses between group companies (except via s.44A group relief, with all its restrictions)
- Parent cannot absorb subsidiary's losses (or vice versa)
- Losses in one subsidiary CANNOT reduce tax in another subsidiary

### 7.2 Strategic Implications

- Groups often structure profitable activities in one entity and loss-making in another → tax inefficient
- Only solution: restructure so profitable and loss-making activities are in the SAME entity (commercial substance required)
- Alternatively: ensure loss company also has other income sources to absorb losses against
- Planning: new ventures should be in existing profitable entity if losses expected in early years

## 8. STAMP DUTY GROUP RELIEF — SECTION 15A (STAMP ACT 1949)

### 8.1 Relief Available

Transfer of property (real property or shares) between **associated companies** qualifies for stamp duty exemption:
- 100% exemption from ad valorem stamp duty
- Applies to conveyances/transfers and share transfers within a group

### 8.2 Conditions

| # | Condition |
|---|---|
| 1 | One company must be beneficial owner of ≥90% of ordinary share capital of the other, OR both must be ≥90% owned by a common holding company |
| 2 | The 90% relationship must exist for at least 12 months before AND 24 months after the transfer |
| 3 | Both companies must be incorporated in Malaysia |
| 4 | Transfer not part of arrangement to transfer to non-associated party |

### 8.3 Clawback

If within **24 months** of the stamp duty-exempt transfer:
- The 90% relationship ceases, OR
- The asset is transferred to a non-associated company

Then:
- Full stamp duty becomes payable (retrospectively) plus penalty
- Both parties jointly and severally liable

### 8.4 Comparison with Income Tax Group Thresholds

| Relief | Ownership Threshold | Additional Key Condition |
|---|---|---|
| Group relief (s.44A, ITA) | 70% | First 3 YAs of surrendering company |
| Controlled transfer (Sch 3, Para 38) | 50% (control) | Joint election, CA assets only |
| Stamp duty exemption (s.15A, Stamp Act) | 90% | Hold for 12m before + 24m after |
| Related party (TP rules, s.140A) | 20% or "control" | Arm's length required |

## 9. GROUP RESTRUCTURING — TAX IMPLICATIONS

### 9.1 Share Acquisitions

- Buying shares in a company: NO stamp duty on listed shares; stamp duty on unlisted shares (0.3%)
- Goodwill/premium: not deductible (capital expenditure)
- CGT: from 1 March 2024, disposal of unlisted shares → CGT applies (2% or 10%)

### 9.2 Asset Acquisitions

- Buying individual assets: stamp duty on real property (1%-4% progressive); no stamp duty on plant/equipment
- Transfer pricing on asset sale from related party: must be at market value (s.140A)
- CA: buyer gets fresh IA + AA on acquisition cost (unless Para 38 controlled transfer elected)
- Balancing charge/allowance: arises for seller

### 9.3 Mergers & Demergers

- Malaysia has no specific tax-neutral merger provisions (unlike UK)
- Mergers: treated as disposal by target + acquisition by acquirer → all normal tax consequences
- Scheme of arrangement (s.366 Companies Act 2016): court approval, but NO automatic tax relief
- Each transfer must be analysed for: income tax, stamp duty, RPGT/CGT, GST/SST

### 9.4 Conversion of Entity Type

| Conversion | Tax Impact |
|---|---|
| Sole prop → Sdn Bhd | Disposal of business: balancing adjustments, stock at MV, B/F losses LOST |
| Partnership → Sdn Bhd | Same as above — ALL assets deemed disposed, new entity starts fresh |
| Sdn Bhd → PLT | s.19(6): same business test may preserve CA if ownership substantially same |
| PLT → Sdn Bhd | Similar — deemed disposal, losses may be lost |

## 10. PRACTICAL CHECKLIST — GROUP TAX REVIEW

```
FOR EACH COMPANY IN A GROUP:

□ Is it an IHC? → Apply s.60F restrictive treatment
□ Any inter-company transactions? → Transfer pricing documentation required
□ Inter-company loans? → Earnings stripping test (20% tax-EBITDA cap)
□ Management fees charged? → Arm's length, real services, benefit test
□ Any recent asset transfers within group? → Para 38 election considered?
□ Any shareholder changes in past 3 years? → Shareholder continuity test
□ New subsidiary in first 3 YAs with losses? → Group relief possible?
□ Any group restructuring planned? → Stamp duty s.15A relief available?
□ Any payments to non-resident group company? → WHT obligations
□ Any Labuan entity in group? → Substance test, TP documentation
□ Foreign subsidiary dividends received? → FSI rules, FTC available?
□ Guarantee given to related party? → Guarantee fee at arm's length?
```
