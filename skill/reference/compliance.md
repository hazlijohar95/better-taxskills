# Edge Cases, Errors, Audits, Refunds & Compliance

Everything that happens when things DON'T go according to plan.

---

## 1. AMENDED RETURNS & ERROR CORRECTION

### 1.1 Error Discovered BEFORE Filing Deadline

```
SITUATION: You realise the computation is wrong before the 7-month deadline.
ACTION: Simply file the CORRECT computation. No penalty. No amended return needed.
         You only get one shot at filing — the first submission IS the assessment.
```

### 1.2 Error Discovered AFTER Filing (Taxpayer's Favour — overpaid)

```
SITUATION: Filed computation had an error that OVERSTATED tax (you paid too much).
ACTION:
  - Write to LHDN requesting review under Section 131
  - Provide:
    □ Revised tax computation showing correct position
    □ Explanation of the error
    □ Supporting documents
  - LHDN may issue a reduced assessment
  - Must apply within 5 YEARS from the date of assessment

TIMELINE: There is no statutory timeline for LHDN to respond.
          In practice: 6-24 months.
```

### 1.3 Error Discovered AFTER Filing (LHDN's Favour — underpaid)

```
SITUATION: Filed computation had an error that UNDERSTATED tax.
OPTIONS:

OPTION A — VOLUNTARY DISCLOSURE:
  - Write to LHDN before they find it
  - Submit revised computation with explanation
  - Pay the additional tax + potential penalty
  - LHDN has discretion on penalty amount
  - Voluntary disclosure SIGNIFICANTLY reduces penalty risk
    (typically 15-45% penalty vs 100% if caught in audit)

OPTION B — DO NOTHING (and hope LHDN doesn't catch it):
  - If caught in audit → Section 113 penalty up to 100% of tax undercharged
  - If wilful → Section 114 penalty up to 300% + criminal prosecution
  - LHDN has 5 years to raise additional assessment (unlimited if fraud)

RECOMMENDATION: Always voluntary disclose. The penalty differential is enormous.
```

### 1.4 Section 131 — Relief from Errors

```
The Director General may:
- Give relief in respect of errors/mistakes in any return
- Reduce the assessment
- Make a repayment

Conditions:
- Taxpayer must make application in writing
- Must be within 5 years of the assessment
- Must demonstrate genuine error (not a change of mind on tax planning)
```

## 2. LHDN AUDIT PROCEDURES

### 2.1 Types of Audit

```
DESK AUDIT (most common):
- LHDN reviews the filed return from their office
- Issues a query letter (usually within 1-3 years of filing)
- Asks for supporting documents for specific items
- You respond in writing with documents
- May result in additional assessment or closure

FIELD AUDIT:
- LHDN officers physically visit the company premises
- Usually for larger companies or suspected significant understatement
- Officers examine books, records, bank statements on-site
- Can take several days
- Officers may interview directors/staff
- Results in either:
  - "No adjustment" (clean bill)
  - Additional assessment with penalties

INVESTIGATION (serious cases):
- Criminal investigation division
- For suspected fraud, evasion, or syndicated schemes
- May involve seizure of documents
- Can result in prosecution (criminal charges)
- Usually preceded by intelligence gathering
```

### 2.2 What Triggers an Audit

```
HIGH RISK FACTORS:
□ Revenue drop but expenses stayed same or increased
□ Consistent losses declared over multiple years (why still operating?)
□ Large fluctuation year-to-year (profit → loss → profit)
□ Capital allowances unusually high relative to revenue
□ Related party transactions (especially cross-border)
□ Industry benchmarking (your margins are below industry average)
□ First-time incentive claims
□ Large one-off deductions
□ Tips/complaints from former employees or business rivals
□ Random selection (LHDN audits ~5% of all returns annually)
□ Transfer pricing issues identified in CbC reports
□ WHT compliance failures identified
□ Discrepancies between Form C and other filings (SST, EPF, SOCSO)
```

### 2.3 Audit Process (Desk Audit)

```
STEP 1: LHDN issues letter requesting documents
  - Usually specific: "Please provide invoices for Account Code 6200,
    breakdown of entertainment expenses, fixed asset register..."
  - Response deadline: usually 21-30 days

STEP 2: You gather documents and respond
  - Provide only what's asked (don't volunteer extra information)
  - Provide clear explanations for each item
  - If item is legitimately deductible, provide evidence

STEP 3: LHDN reviews and may:
  (a) Accept your explanation → case closed, no adjustment
  (b) Issue FURTHER queries → another round of correspondence
  (c) Propose adjustments → "We intend to raise additional assessment of RM XXX"
  (d) Raise additional assessment immediately (Section 91)

STEP 4: If adjustments proposed:
  - You can AGREE (pay the additional tax + penalty)
  - You can NEGOTIATE (provide counter-arguments, offer partial agreement)
  - You can DISAGREE (LHDN raises assessment, you then appeal)

TYPICAL TIMELINE: 6 months to 3 years from first query to resolution
```

### 2.4 Audit Powers of LHDN

```
Section 78: Power to call for returns, accounts, information
Section 79: Power to call for SPECIFIC returns (any person)
Section 80: Power of access to buildings, documents
Section 81: Power to examine persons on oath
Section 82: Obligation to keep proper records (7 years)

LHDN CAN:
- Access any premises where business is conducted
- Inspect/copy/seize any books, accounts, records
- Examine any person (under oath) regarding the affairs of any taxpayer
- Require banks to disclose information about accounts
- Require any person to attend and give evidence

LHDN CANNOT:
- Force entry without warrant (for criminal investigations)
- Act beyond the 5-year statutory window (unless fraud)
- Raise assessment on items already assessed (functus officio)
```

## 3. ADDITIONAL ASSESSMENTS (RAISED BY LHDN)

### 3.1 Section 91 — Additional Assessment

```
WHEN: LHDN believes tax has been undercharged in any YA
WHO: Director General or any authorised officer
TIMELINE: Within 5 years of the relevant YA (no limit if fraud/wilful default/negligence)

WHAT HAPPENS:
1. LHDN issues Notice of Additional Assessment
2. Shows the additional tax charged
3. Shows the penalty (if any)
4. Due date for payment (usually 30 days from notice)

THE TAXPAYER MUST:
- Either PAY the amount, OR
- File a NOTICE OF APPEAL within 30 days to the Special Commissioners of Income Tax
- Payment is NOT suspended during appeal unless specific application is made
```

### 3.2 Penalties on Additional Assessments

```
Section 113(2) — Incorrect Return:
  LHDN may impose penalty not exceeding the amount of tax undercharged.
  In practice:
    - First offence: 45% of tax undercharged (LHDN's published guideline)
    - Second offence: 45% to 100%
    - Voluntary disclosure before audit: 15% (LHDN's incentive programme)

Section 112(3) — Failure to Furnish Return:
  20% to 35% of tax payable (administrative penalty)
  + Prosecution: RM200-RM20,000 fine or ≤ 6 months jail

Section 114 — Wilful Evasion:
  RM1,000-RM20,000 fine or ≤ 3 years imprisonment
  + Special penalty of 300% of tax undercharged
  (This is criminal — requires prosecution, burden of proof on LHDN)
```

### 3.3 LHDN Voluntary Disclosure Programme

```
LHDN periodically offers reduced penalties for voluntary disclosure:

STANDARD VOLUNTARY DISCLOSURE (ongoing):
- Taxpayer comes forward before LHDN initiates audit
- Typical penalty: 15% of additional tax (significantly below 45%)
- Must be genuine voluntary (not prompted by audit letter)

SPECIAL PROGRAMMES (periodic):
- LHDN occasionally runs amnesty programmes (e.g., SVDP 2018/2019)
- Reduced penalties (sometimes 0% on penalty, just pay the tax)
- Check if any active programme exists at time of disclosure
```

## 4. OBJECTIONS AND APPEALS

### 4.1 Dispute Process Flow

```
LHDN raises assessment
        │
        ▼
TAXPAYER DISAGREES
        │
        ▼
File FORM Q (Notice of Appeal) within 30 DAYS
to Special Commissioners of Income Tax
        │
        ▼
Special Commissioners hearing (quasi-judicial)
  - Both parties present evidence
  - Decision binding unless appealed
        │
        ▼
EITHER PARTY unhappy with decision
        │
        ▼
Appeal to HIGH COURT on point of law (within 21 days)
        │
        ▼
Appeal to COURT OF APPEAL (within 30 days)
        │
        ▼
Final appeal to FEDERAL COURT (with leave)
```

### 4.2 Form Q — Notice of Appeal

```
DEADLINE: 30 days from date of Notice of Assessment / Additional Assessment
FILED TO: Special Commissioners of Income Tax (SCIT)
CONTENT:
  - Grounds of appeal (why the assessment is wrong)
  - Amount in dispute
  - Supporting arguments

IMPORTANT:
  - Late filing of Form Q = appeal INVALID (strict deadline)
  - Missing deadline = assessment becomes FINAL AND CONCLUSIVE
  - Payment of tax is NOT automatically stayed during appeal
    (must apply separately for stay of payment)
```

### 4.3 Stay of Payment During Appeal

```
Section 103(1A): Taxpayer can apply to DGIR for stay
  - Must demonstrate genuine dispute and hardship
  - DGIR has discretion
  - If refused, can apply to SCIT
  - If granted, tax payment deferred until appeal resolved
  - Interest may still accrue
```

## 5. REFUNDS

### 5.1 When Refunds Arise

```
SCENARIO 1: CP204 OVERPAYMENT
  - Instalments paid exceed actual tax liability
  - Automatic refund (in theory) after filing Form C
  - LHDN processes within 30 working days (statutory)
  - Reality: can take 3-12 months

SCENARIO 2: SECTION 110 CREDIT EXCESS
  - Withholding tax deducted at source exceeds tax liability
  - Refund after filing

SCENARIO 3: AMENDED ASSESSMENT (taxpayer's favour)
  - After successful appeal or Section 131 application
  - LHDN refunds the excess paid

SCENARIO 4: DOUBLE TAX RELIEF EXCESS
  - Foreign tax credit (Section 132/bilateral) exceeds tax liability
```

### 5.2 Refund Procedure

```
AUTOMATIC REFUND:
1. File Form C showing tax refundable position
2. LHDN's system processes the return
3. If no outstanding tax liabilities under ANY tax act:
   → Refund credited to company bank account on LHDN's file
4. If outstanding liabilities exist:
   → LHDN offsets refund against outstanding amount first
   → Balance (if any) refunded

MANUAL REFUND (amended/appeal):
1. LHDN issues revised notice of assessment
2. Taxpayer writes to request refund
3. LHDN processes (longer timeline)
```

### 5.3 Refund Delays — What To Do

```
IF refund exceeds 30 working days (Form C/B):
  - Call LHDN customer service (03-8911 1000)
  - Check status on MyTax portal
  - Write formal letter to branch requesting expedition

IF refund exceeds 6 months:
  - File complaint via LHDN complaint channel
  - Engage tax agent to follow up
  - In extreme cases: write to Hasil branch director

COMMON CAUSES OF DELAY:
  - Bank account details incorrect/outdated on LHDN's file
  - Outstanding filings for other YAs blocking the refund
  - LHDN internal review/audit flag on the return
  - System backlog (especially post-filing season)
  - Company has outstanding SST/WHT/PCB liabilities
```

### 5.4 Interest on Late Refunds

```
Section 111D: Where refund is not made within the prescribed period,
LHDN shall pay interest at 2% per annum on the refund amount.

In practice: LHDN rarely pays this voluntarily.
The taxpayer must often request it explicitly.
```

## 6. CHANGE OF FINANCIAL YEAR END

### 6.1 Impact on Tax

```
SITUATION: Company changes FYE (e.g., from 31 Dec to 30 June)

IMPLICATIONS:
- Transition period creates a SHORT BASIS PERIOD (< 12 months)
  OR a LONG BASIS PERIOD (> 12 months)
- Each YA must have only ONE basis period

RULE:
- If FYE changes, the new basis period is allocated to the YA
  in which the new FYE falls
- A YA cannot have two basis periods
- A short period still gets ONE set of CP204 (pro-rated instalments)
```

### 6.2 Short Basis Period

```
< 12 MONTHS:
- Capital allowances: IA given in full; AA pro-rated (months/12)
- Loss carry-forward: full year counts as one of the 10 years
- CP204: if < 6 months, company exempt from CP204 for that period
- Small value asset cap: still RM20,000 (not pro-rated)
```

### 6.3 Long Basis Period (> 12 months)

```
This should NOT happen. If it does, LHDN treats it as:
- Two basis periods (one ending on the old date, one on the new)
- Each assessed separately
- Can result in two YAs being assessed in one calendar year
```

## 7. COMMENCEMENT AND CESSATION

### 7.1 Commencement of Business

```
FIRST YEAR:
- Basis period = date business commences to first FYE
- May be short period (e.g., incorporate March, FYE December = 10 months)
- Pre-commencement expenses: deductible in first YA
- CP204: exempt for first 2 years if paid-up capital ≤ RM2.5m
- IA given on assets acquired in the basis period
- No B/F losses or CA (obviously)

COMMON TRAP:
- Company incorporated but not yet trading = not yet "commenced business"
- Business commences when FIRST business transaction occurs
  (not date of incorporation, not date of first invoice)
- LHDN may challenge: "When did you actually start?"
```

### 7.2 Cessation of Business

```
FINAL YEAR:
- All assets disposed of → balancing adjustments on every asset
  - Assets sold: disposal value = sale price
  - Assets retained by owner (not sold): disposal value = market value
  - Assets scrapped: disposal value = scrap value (or nil)
- Unabsorbed losses: LOST (cannot be carried forward past cessation)
- Unabsorbed CA: LOST
- Stock-in-trade: deemed sold at market value (taxable)
- Work-in-progress: deemed completed at market value

FILING:
- Must still file Form C for the final period
- Deadline: still 7 months from cessation date
- Must notify LHDN of cessation

COMMON TRAP:
- Company struck off SSM but forgot to file final Form C
  → LHDN can still assess + impose penalties
- Dormant company ≠ ceased company (see below)
```

### 7.3 Dormant Companies

```
DEFINITION: Company that has NO business transactions during the basis period.
            Still exists on SSM register but not trading.

TAX IMPLICATIONS:
- Must still file Form C every year (declaring nil income)
- If fails to file: penalties apply
- B/F losses: preserved BUT subject to shareholders' continuity test
  - If > 50% change in shareholders during dormant period:
    → All B/F losses and unabsorbed CA are FORFEITED
    → This is the "substantial change in shareholders" rule (Para 75A-75C)
- When trading resumes: can utilise preserved losses (if no shareholder change)
- LHDN may challenge: "Was the company truly dormant or was there hidden income?"

CP204:
- Dormant company: submit NIL estimate
- If LHDN doesn't accept NIL: write formal representation
```

## 8. SHAREHOLDER CONTINUITY TEST

### 8.1 The Rule (Paragraphs 75A-75C, Schedule 3 + Section 44(5C))

```
IF there is a SUBSTANTIAL CHANGE in shareholders of a company:
  → Unabsorbed business losses are FORFEITED
  → Unabsorbed capital allowances are FORFEITED

SUBSTANTIAL CHANGE means:
  More than 50% of the paid-up capital is held by persons
  who DID NOT hold it at the time the losses/CA were incurred.

WHEN THIS MATTERS:
  - Company acquisition (new shareholder buys >50%)
  - Restructuring (shares transferred to new holding company)
  - Dormant company being "revived" by new owners
  - Any change where different persons control the company
```

### 8.2 Exception

```
Change is NOT treated as substantial if:
  - The business carried on is the SAME business as before
  - The change is mainly for reasons other than obtaining tax benefits
  - The business has not undergone a major change in nature/character

In practice: This exception is VERY narrowly applied by LHDN.
If >50% shares change hands, expect losses/CA to be challenged.
```

## 9. MULTIPLE BUSINESS SOURCES

### 9.1 When Does a Company Have More Than One Source?

```
RULE: Generally, a Sdn Bhd has ONE business source (Section 4(a)) regardless
      of how many different activities it conducts.

EXCEPTION: Where the businesses are so distinct that they constitute
           SEPARATE and DISTINCT sources:
  - Different industry/nature entirely
  - Separate books and records
  - Would logically be separate companies

IN PRACTICE: LHDN rarely argues multiple sources for a company.
             The issue matters because:
  - Unabsorbed CA can only offset income from the SAME source
  - If only one source: all CA offsets against that one pool
  - If multiple sources: CA from source A cannot offset source B profit
```

### 9.2 Investment Holding Company (IHC)

```
A company whose MAIN activity is holding investments (shares, property).

TAX TREATMENT:
- Income is passive (dividends, interest, rental)
- Section 60F: Only expenses DIRECTLY related to each investment income source
  are deductible against that source
- NO business source (Section 4(a)) unless actively trading
- Directors' fees, admin costs: must be apportioned across income sources
- Unabsorbed expenses: CANNOT be carried forward (no business loss rules)

COMMON TRAP: IHC claiming business deductions (salaries, office costs)
            against rental income — LHDN will disallow if not directly related.
```

## 10. REAL PROPERTY GAINS TAX (RPGT)

### 10.1 Separate Regime

```
RPGT is a SEPARATE tax from income tax. Different rates, different form.
But the AI agent must know when it applies because:
  - Disposal of land/building by a company → RPGT (not income tax)
  - Unless company is a PROPERTY DEVELOPER (then it's business income under s.4(a))
```

### 10.2 Current RPGT Rates (from 2024)

```
COMPANIES:
  - Disposal within 3 years of acquisition: 30%
  - Disposal in year 4: 20%
  - Disposal in year 5: 15%
  - Disposal in year 6 and beyond: 10%

INDIVIDUALS (citizen/PR):
  - Within 3 years: 30%
  - Year 4: 20%
  - Year 5: 15%
  - Year 6 onwards: 0% (exempt)

NON-CITIZENS:
  - Within 5 years: 30%
  - Year 6 onwards: 10%
```

### 10.3 When is it Income Tax instead of RPGT?

```
IF the company is in the BUSINESS of property development/trading:
  → Gains on disposal = Section 4(a) business income
  → Subject to income tax (24%)
  → NOT RPGT

IF the company holds property as INVESTMENT (long-term capital):
  → Gains on disposal = chargeable gain under RPGT Act
  → Subject to RPGT rates above
  → NOT income tax

The distinction: INTENTION at time of purchase + badges of trade
```

## 11. CAPITAL GAINS TAX ON SHARES (from 1 March 2024)

### 11.1 When It Applies

```
Disposal of UNLISTED shares in a Malaysian company on or after 1 March 2024.

RATES:
- Shares acquired BEFORE 1 March 2024:
  → Flat rate of 2% on GROSS disposal price (no deduction for cost)

- Shares acquired ON OR AFTER 1 March 2024:
  → 10% on NET GAIN (disposal price minus acquisition cost)

EXEMPTIONS:
- Listed shares (traded on Bursa Malaysia): EXEMPT
- Shares in approved venture capital companies: EXEMPT
- Restructuring within same group (conditions apply): EXEMPT
- Individual: first RM10,000 gain exempt (for shares acquired on/after 1 March 2024)
```

### 11.2 Interaction with Tax Computation

```
CGT on shares is SEPARATE from income tax computation.
It does NOT appear in the Section 4(a) adjusted income.
It is filed separately.

BUT: If company is in the BUSINESS of share trading:
  → Gains = Section 4(a) business income (income tax, not CGT)
  → Loss = deductible business loss
```

## 12. TRANSFER PRICING DISPUTES

### 12.1 Documentation Requirements

```
Companies with RELATED PARTY TRANSACTIONS must maintain
contemporaneous transfer pricing documentation:

MANDATORY IF:
  - Gross income > RM25 million AND related party transactions > RM15 million
  - OR: financial assistance > RM50 million with related parties

DOCUMENTATION MUST INCLUDE:
  - Organisational structure
  - Nature of business
  - Controlled transactions (type, quantum)
  - Comparability analysis
  - Selection of transfer pricing method
  - Application of method
  - Supporting evidence

IF NOT MAINTAINED:
  → 5% surcharge on TP adjustments (on top of additional tax)
  → Penalty under Section 113 (up to 100% of tax shortfall)
```

### 12.2 Common TP Adjustments

```
LHDN COMMONLY CHALLENGES:
  - Management fees paid to foreign parent (are they arm's length?)
  - Royalties to related parties (is the rate benchmarked?)
  - Inter-company loans at non-market interest rates
  - Sale of goods to related parties below market price
  - Cost allocation/recharges without proper basis

IF ADJUSTED:
  - Additional taxable income = amount adjusted to arm's length
  - Tax + 5% surcharge on adjustment + Section 113 penalty
  - Double taxation risk (other country may not give corresponding relief)
  - Advance Pricing Arrangement (APA) available to mitigate future risk
```

## 13. COMMON ERRORS IN TAX COMPUTATIONS

### 13.1 Errors That LHDN Catches Most Often

```
1. DEPRECIATION NOT FULLY ADDED BACK
   - Company has multiple depreciation lines in accounts
   - Tax agent misses one (e.g., amortisation of intangibles)
   - LHDN sees depreciation in accounts ≠ add-back in computation

2. GENERAL PROVISION TREATED AS SPECIFIC BAD DEBT
   - "Provision for doubtful debts" based on % of debtors
   - Tax agent claims as deduction
   - LHDN disallows — it's general, not specific

3. CAPITAL EXPENDITURE DEDUCTED AS REVENUE
   - Renovation treated as "repair"
   - New equipment expensed instead of capitalised
   - LHDN's test: did it create an enduring benefit?

4. ENTERTAINMENT NOT RESTRICTED
   - All entertainment deducted at 100%
   - Only proviso items qualify for 100%
   - Rest is 50% only

5. WRONG SME STATUS
   - Company has corporate shareholder with paid-up > RM2.5m
   - Or gross income exceeded RM50m
   - Applied SME rates when should have been flat 24%

6. LOSSES CARRIED FORWARD BEYOND 10 YEARS
   - Loss from YA 2014 claimed in YA 2025 (11 years)
   - Only 10 years from YA 2019 onwards (transitional rules apply)

7. DONATIONS EXCEEDING CAP
   - Total donations claimed exceed 10% of aggregate income
   - Or claimed donation to non-approved body

8. WHT NOT DEDUCTED — EXPENSE STILL CLAIMED
   - Paid technical fees to Singapore consultant
   - Didn't deduct 10% WHT
   - Still claimed the expense as deductible
   - LHDN disallows under Section 39(2)

9. MOTOR VEHICLE QE NOT CAPPED
   - Full cost of RM250,000 car used as QE
   - Should be capped at RM100,000 (or RM50,000)
   - IA and AA overclaimed

10. RELATED PARTY TRANSACTIONS NOT ARM'S LENGTH
    - Management fees at 5% of revenue to parent company
    - No benchmarking, no TP documentation
    - LHDN adjusts to nil or lower rate
```

### 13.2 Errors That Harm The Client (overpaid tax)

```
1. DOUBLE DEDUCTIONS NOT CLAIMED
   - Training qualifies for 200% but only claimed at 100%
   - Export promotion expenses not identified as double deduction
   - Disabled employee remuneration not flagged

2. CAPITAL ALLOWANCES MISSED
   - New assets purchased but not added to CA schedule
   - Small value assets not claimed (left in fixed asset register)
   - Budget 2026 ACA not applied (local machinery, ICT)

3. PRIOR YEAR LOSSES NOT UTILISED
   - B/F losses exist but not offset (oversight)
   - Or offset against wrong source

4. RENOVATION ALLOWANCE NOT CLAIMED
   - Qualifying renovation expenditure exists
   - But not claimed (RM300,000 cap available)

5. INCENTIVES NOT APPLIED FOR
   - Company qualifies for RA but never applied to MIDA
   - Automation CA available but not claimed

6. WRONG RATE APPLIED
   - Company qualifies for SME rate but flat 24% applied
   - (Opposite of error #5 above)

7. ZAKAT NOT DEDUCTED
   - Company paid zakat but didn't claim Section 44(11A) deduction
   - Cap is 2.5% of aggregate income
```

## 14. LHDN CORRESPONDENCE HANDLING

### 14.1 Types of LHDN Letters

```
TYPE 1: QUERY LETTER (Surat Pertanyaan)
  - "Please explain/provide documents for item X in your return"
  - Must respond within 21-30 days (stated in letter)
  - Failure to respond → LHDN may raise assessment based on best judgment

TYPE 2: PROPOSED ADJUSTMENT LETTER
  - "We propose to adjust your assessment by RM XXX"
  - Usually includes breakdown of items adjusted
  - Taxpayer has right to respond/object before formal assessment

TYPE 3: NOTICE OF ADDITIONAL ASSESSMENT (Notis Taksiran Tambahan)
  - Formal. This IS the additional assessment.
  - Must pay within 30 days OR file Form Q appeal within 30 days
  - Clock starts ticking immediately

TYPE 4: PENALTY NOTICE (Notis Penalti)
  - Late filing penalty
  - Underestimation penalty (CP204)
  - Usually accompanies or follows an assessment

TYPE 5: REMINDER LETTER (Peringatan)
  - "You have outstanding balance of RM XXX"
  - Pay immediately or contact LHDN
  - Precedes enforcement action
```

### 14.2 Response Strategy

```
GENERAL PRINCIPLES:
1. ALWAYS respond within the deadline
2. Never ignore LHDN correspondence (escalates rapidly)
3. Provide only what's asked — don't volunteer extra information
4. Keep copies of everything sent and received
5. Send via registered post or LHDN portal (proof of delivery)
6. If you need more time: write to request extension BEFORE deadline

IF YOU AGREE with adjustment:
  → Write acknowledging, pay the additional tax + penalty
  → Case closed quickly

IF YOU PARTIALLY AGREE:
  → Write explaining which items you accept and which you dispute
  → Provide evidence for disputed items
  → LHDN may negotiate

IF YOU DISAGREE COMPLETELY:
  → Write detailed explanation with evidence
  → If LHDN still raises assessment → file Form Q within 30 days
  → Engage tax lawyer if amount is material
```

## 15. ENFORCEMENT AND COLLECTION

### 15.1 What Happens if Tax Not Paid

```
ESCALATION TIMELINE:
Day 1: Tax is due (7 months from FYE)
Day 2: Tax unpaid → 10% penalty automatically imposed
Day 60: Still unpaid → FURTHER 5% penalty on total (including first 10%)
Day 61+: LHDN sends reminder notices
Day 90+: LHDN may:
  - Issue travel restriction (Section 104) — cannot leave Malaysia
  - Issue garnishee order to banks (Section 106) — freeze accounts
  - Issue notice to third parties (Section 104A) — debtors pay LHDN directly
  - Take civil recovery action in court (Section 106)
  - In extreme cases: bankruptcy/winding-up proceedings

SECTION 104 — TRAVEL BAN:
  - DGIR can direct the Immigration Department to prevent departure
  - Applied against directors (not just company)
  - Lifted only when tax paid or arrangement made

SECTION 106 — CIVIL PROCEEDINGS:
  - LHDN files suit in court to recover tax as a debt
  - Court judgment → execution (seizure of assets)
```

### 15.2 Instalment Arrangements

```
IF company cannot pay full amount at once:
  - Write to LHDN branch requesting instalment arrangement
  - Propose: amount per month × number of months
  - LHDN has discretion to accept or reject
  - If accepted: must strictly comply with schedule
  - Missing one payment → arrangement cancelled, full balance due immediately
  - Penalties still apply (10% + 5% already imposed don't get reversed)
  - Travel ban may still be imposed even with arrangement in place
```

## 16. SPECIAL SITUATIONS

### 16.1 Company Under Liquidation

```
LIQUIDATOR'S OBLIGATIONS:
  - Must file all outstanding returns (past and current year)
  - Must obtain TAX CLEARANCE from LHDN before final distribution
  - Must pay all outstanding tax before distributing assets
  - LHDN has priority over unsecured creditors for tax debts

PROCEDURE:
1. Liquidator appointed (by court or members' resolution)
2. Liquidator assesses tax position
3. Files any outstanding returns
4. Pays outstanding tax (or makes provision)
5. Applies to LHDN for tax clearance letter
6. LHDN issues clearance (or raises outstanding assessments first)
7. Only AFTER clearance: final distribution to shareholders
8. Company struck off

COMMON TRAP: Distributing assets before tax clearance → liquidator personally liable
```

### 16.2 Company Strike-Off (Section 308, Companies Act 2016)

```
BEFORE applying to SSM for strike-off:
  □ File all outstanding tax returns (Form C for every year)
  □ Ensure no outstanding tax liabilities
  □ Obtain tax clearance letter from LHDN (not always mandatory but recommended)
  □ Cancel CP204 obligations

IF struck off WITHOUT filing:
  - LHDN can still raise assessments
  - Directors may be personally liable
  - SSM may reject strike-off if LHDN objects
```

### 16.3 Merger / Acquisition

```
ACQUIRING COMPANY BUYS >50% SHARES:
  - Target company's B/F losses and unabsorbed CA may be FORFEITED
    (shareholder continuity test)
  - Must check if same business continues (exception may apply)
  - Due diligence should quantify tax losses at risk

BUSINESS TRANSFER (assets, not shares):
  - Seller: balancing adjustments on all assets transferred
  - Buyer: fresh QE at purchase price (may be higher or lower than tax WDV)
  - Stock transferred: at market value (taxable for seller)
  - No transfer of losses (they stay with the seller entity)
```

### 16.4 Change of Tax Agent

```
WHEN NEW TAX AGENT TAKES OVER:
  - Obtain Form CP55 authority from client
  - Register as authorised agent with LHDN
  - Obtain prior year computations from outgoing agent (client must request)
  - Obtain B/F loss memo and CA schedule
  - Verify prior year position independently (don't blindly trust previous work)
  - Check if any LHDN queries outstanding

COMMON ISSUE: Outgoing agent doesn't cooperate.
  → Client must exercise their legal right to obtain their own records
  → Reconstruct CA schedule from fixed asset register + prior Form C filings
```

### 16.5 Deceased Sole Proprietor

```
DEATH DURING BASIS PERIOD:
  - Business income assessable up to date of death
  - Executor/administrator files Form B for the deceased (final return)
  - Personal reliefs still claimable for that year
  - Business losses: lost (cannot transfer to estate beneficiaries)
  - Capital allowances: last year's CA computed up to date of death
  - Balancing adjustments if business assets distributed/sold

EXECUTOR'S OBLIGATIONS:
  - File all outstanding returns
  - Pay tax from estate assets
  - Obtain tax clearance before distributing estate
```

## 17. E-INVOICE REQUIREMENTS (from 2024-2025)

### 17.1 Mandatory Implementation Timeline

```
Phase 1 (1 Aug 2024): Companies with turnover > RM100 million
Phase 2 (1 Jan 2025): Companies with turnover > RM25 million
Phase 3 (1 Jul 2025): ALL taxpayers (including individuals with business income)

NON-COMPLIANCE:
  - Deduction of expense may be DISALLOWED if no valid e-invoice obtained
  - Fine up to RM50,000 or imprisonment up to 3 years (Section 120 ITA)
```

### 17.2 Impact on Tax Computation

```
FROM PHASE 3 ONWARDS:
  - Every expense claimed as deduction should be supported by a valid e-invoice
  - Self-billed e-invoices required for certain transactions
  - Tax agent must verify e-invoice compliance when preparing computation
  - If expense has no valid e-invoice: technically non-deductible
  - Transitional relief may apply (check current LHDN guidelines)
```

## 18. VALIDATION MATRIX FOR THE AI AGENT

The AI agent should check ALL of these before finalising any computation:

```
STRUCTURAL CHECKS:
□ Adjusted income + add-backs - deductions = net profit per accounts
□ CA schedule residual b/f + QE - IA - AA = residual c/f (per asset)
□ Statutory income ≥ 0 (per source)
□ Chargeable income ≥ 0
□ Total add-backs are reasonable relative to net profit
□ Prior year B/F figures match what was actually filed

RATE/THRESHOLD CHECKS:
□ SME status correctly determined (all 5 conditions checked)
□ Correct rate bands applied
□ Donation cap: 10% of aggregate income
□ Zakat cap: 2.5% of aggregate income (companies)
□ Motor vehicle QE capped correctly
□ Small value assets within annual cap
□ Loss B/F within 10-year limit (check year of origin)

COMPLIANCE CHECKS:
□ WHT obligations identified and verified as remitted
□ Related party transactions flagged for TP documentation
□ CP204 comparison (will underestimation penalty apply?)
□ No shareholder changes that would forfeit losses/CA
□ e-Invoice requirements noted for expense deductions
□ Filing deadline calculated correctly

RED FLAGS (flag for human review):
□ Adjusted income is NEGATIVE (loss) — is this genuine?
□ Capital allowances exceed adjusted income — confirm sources
□ Related party transactions > 50% of expenses
□ First-time incentive claim
□ Change in accounting policy/FYE
□ Large one-off items (> 20% of normal profit)
□ Company previously dormant now profitable (shareholder change?)
□ Forex gains/losses > 10% of profit (verify trade vs non-trade)
□ Motor vehicle cost > RM150,000
□ Any payment to non-resident > RM50,000
```
